# Style sheet: NeuroImage research article

Derived from the NeuroImage Guide for Authors (saved 2026-10-05; it overrides the papers) and ten 2024–2026 NeuroImage MEG/OPM-MEG papers (deposited accepted manuscripts): Matsubara 2026; Núñez Ponasso 2025; Wartman 2025; Arif 2025; Youssofzadeh 2026; Jiao 2024; Ward 2025; Pulliam 2024; Jia 2025; Wang 2025. Majority conventions are followed; the split is given where the papers disagree.

**Structure.** Number sections and subsections (1., 1.1., 1.1.1.); the abstract is unnumbered; cross-refer by number ("Section 2.3"). Use 1. Introduction, 2. Materials and methods, 3. Results, 4. Discussion, 5. Conclusions. End the Introduction with the aims (exploratory aims stated as such). Order Results subsections as the Methods. Open the Discussion with an unnumbered summary paragraph, then numbered thematic subsections; Limitations is the last subsection and a single paragraph. Conclusions: one paragraph, 70–160 words, no citations. Back matter in this order: CRediT authorship contribution statement; Declaration of competing interest; Funding; Data and code availability; Ethics statement; Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process; References; Appendix A (Supplementary material). No bullet lists in the text.

**Front matter.** Concise title; authors with lowercase superscript affiliation letters and full postal addresses; one corresponding author with email; no acknowledgements on the title page. Highlights: 3–5 bullets, each ≤85 characters with spaces, also supplied as a separate file. Abstract: one unstructured paragraph, ≤250 words, moves context → gap → "Here, we …" → design → results with their key numbers and conditions → implication; define abbreviations, cite nothing. Keywords: 5–7, full term with the abbreviation in parentheses, none joined by "and"/"of".

**Tense and person.** Use "we". Methods: past tense (present for definitions and equations). Results: past tense for findings, present for what a figure shows. Discussion: present tense for interpretation. Abstract: present for context and conclusion, past for methods and results.

**Claim strength.** State results plainly ("was", "exceeded"); hedge interpretations once ("may", "likely", "suggest"), not every sentence. Tie claims to the model ("in the simulated heads"); "suggest", not "demonstrate", for patients. No "first", "novel", "conclusive". Report null results plainly. All caveats go in the Limitations paragraph ("First … Finally"), each with the test that would resolve it; elsewhere a condition is stated once where it matters.

**Length.** Sentences: mean 25–28 words, rarely above 45 (papers: means 26–37, median 29). Paragraphs: 70–120 words, one point each (papers: median 109).

**Statistics in text.** Put the statistic in parentheses after the claim. Give point estimates with 95 % intervals in brackets, "1.14 [1.12, 1.17]", and say once what the interval describes. Name the test, the correction (Holm), α and the number of replicates. Write p with a leading zero (p = 0.010; 3/7 papers, 4/7 omit it), "p < 0.0001" as the floor. Proportions as 8 of 9 or 67/74 (90.5 %). Ratios and dB to two decimals; percentages to whole numbers unless smaller.

**Figures.** One file per figure (Figure_1.pdf …), vector PDF for plots, embedded raster ≥300 dpi (line art ≥1000 dpi); cited in order as "Fig. 3A", "Figs. 4–5" (never "Figure" in running text). Panels: bold uppercase letters at top left (8/10). Caption: "Fig. N." + short title phrase, then (A) … (B) …, then what bars and intervals show, n, and abbreviations; explain every symbol. Axis labels "Quantity (unit)". Keep each array's and head's colour fixed across figures; colour-blind-safe palettes, no jet.

**Tables.** Editable text, cited in order. Caption above: "Table N." + title. Horizontal rules only (top, header, bottom), no shading. Units in parentheses in column headers. Notes and abbreviations below the body. Do not repeat values that a figure shows.

**References.** Author–year: (Boto et al., 2017), (Hill et al., 2019; Zahran et al., 2022), "Jas et al. (2026)"; two authors "Taulu and Simola (2006)"; group citations alphabetically, then by year; a/b suffixes. List alphabetically: Surname, A.B., Surname, C., Year. Title in sentence case. Abbrev. J. Vol, pages or article number. https://doi.org/…. Datasets: [dataset] Authors, Year. Title. Repository, version. DOI. Mark preprints. Cite software with its version.

**Units, numbers, terms.** SI units with a space before the unit and before % ("10 mm", "21 %"). Ranges: en dash for unsigned ranges ("1.27–1.47"), "to" for signed ones ("−0.09 to +0.33"). True minus sign, × for products, comma thousands separators, numerals for measurements, words for counts below ten in prose; spell out a number that starts a sentence. Define abbreviations at first use in the abstract and again in the body; define configuration labels once and reuse them unchanged. Number display equations (1) and cite "Eq. (1)", each followed by "where …". Italic variables, solidus for small fractions. American spelling throughout.

**Declarations.** CRediT roles per author; competing interests (including "none"); funding (or the standard no-specific-grant sentence); data and code availability with repository links (Option C); ethics for the source datasets and the absence of new data; SAGER sex/gender statement or limitation; the generative-AI declaration when AI tools were used.
