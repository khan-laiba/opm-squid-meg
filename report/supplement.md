## A. Estimands and conventions {#s-estimands}

**The primary metric.** Known-topography detectability is d = √(sᵀC⁻¹s): the output signal-to-noise ratio of the optimal matched filter for a source of known field pattern s in noise of covariance C. It is computed with the true (oracle) covariance and rank-aware whitening for every target and every array. Arrays are compared target by target. The adult comparison reports the median over targets of the paired ratio d~OPM~/d~Neuromag~, not the ratio of the two systems' median detectabilities. D = 20 log~10~(d~OPM~/d~Neuromag~) is the same quantity in dB, positive when the OPM is ahead. D does not depend on the dipole moment. The comparator is Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels unless stated.

**Two medians, one metric.** The adult analyses report the unweighted median over all {{ F.g2_n_targets }} targets, the {{ F.g2_medialwall_n }} on the medial wall included. The smaller heads use an area-weighted median over cortical targets, the medial wall excluded, because their cortices differ in size and their targets in density. At the adult's measured head position the dense array's ratio is {{ F.g3b_link_g2_ratio_all_targets_3dp }} unweighted over all targets, {{ F.g3b_link_g2_ratio_cortical_3dp }} without the medial wall and, area-weighted without it, {{ F.wr_adult_dense_ib_ratio_area_weighted_cortical_3dp }}; its D is {{ F.wr_adult_dense_ib_db }} dB under the adult convention and {{ F.g3b_link_g2_db }} dB under the pediatric one. A 1 % change of the ratio is {{ F.wr_db_per_percent }} dB, and 1 dB is a ratio of {{ F.wr_ratio_per_db }}. [@stab-adult-both-forms] gives the adult's primary comparison in both forms.

**The reference placement of the adult.** Every Δ is taken against the adult placed by the same rule, because the adult's own D depends on the placement: {{ F.g3b_adult_centred_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB at its measured head position, {{ F.g3b_adult_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB at top contact, {{ F.g3b_adult_xcentred_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB laterally centred and raised to contact, {{ F.g3b_adult_top18mm_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB at true contact, {{ F.g3b_adult_cf_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} and {{ F.g3b_adult_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB in its own helmets scaled with the head (centred and laterally centred; their scale factor is {{ F.g3b_adult_cfx_k }}), and {{ F.rev_cgap_adult_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB in the helmet fitted at its own gap. Where a difference of two pooled medians is used instead of Δ, the text says so. Δ is reported descriptively, without a test or a margin.

**Peak-channel and mean-power SNR.** Peak-channel SNR lets magnetometers and gradiometers compete on equal terms, because each channel is divided by its own noise. It is the closest analogue of the clinical studies' sensor-level SNRs, with one difference: a clinical baseline standard deviation holds physiological noise as well as sensor and room noise, whereas the model's holds only what the noise model contains. Mean-power SNR is the channel-averaged SNR of Goldenholz et al. (2009), also used by Matsubara et al. (2026).

**Plug-in detectability.** The plug-in filter is built from a Ledoit–Wolf covariance estimate (Ledoit & Wolf, 2004) and evaluated under the true covariance, so it cannot exceed the oracle value. The estimate uses {{ F.g2_plugin10s_samples }} or {{ F.g2_plugin60s_samples }} independent samples, the time-bandwidth equivalents of {{ F.g2_cov_seconds }} s of data in this band, drawn for all arrays from one common noise realization. The estimate from {{ F.g2_plugin60s_samples }} samples is the declared {{ F.wr_cov_seconds_long }}-s scenario (independent samples, which do not establish the effective sample size of a measured coloured-noise baseline); the one from {{ F.g2_plugin10s_samples }} samples shows how a short baseline penalizes the array with more channels.

**Intervals.** Neighbouring cortical targets are correlated, so no interval resamples targets as if they were independent, except the adult's frequency-band comparison, which resamples targets and therefore has narrower intervals than the primary comparison ([@stab-resamples]). All intervals are percentile intervals. In the spike study the location is the statistical unit: S50 ratios carry location-bootstrap intervals, and detection differences are tested with the sign-flip test on per-location differences in detection counts, reported exactly over all sign patterns (section G.5). The resample count of every interval family is in [@stab-resamples].

## B. The spherical benchmark {#s-sphere}

**Reimplementation.** The analytical model of Jas et al. (2026) was reimplemented with their definitions: a tangential current dipole of {{ F.g1a_q_nam }} nAm in a spherical conductor (head radius {{ F.g1a_h_mm }} mm, brain radius {{ F.g1a_b_mm }} mm), point magnetometers measuring the radial field on the scalp (OPM) and {{ F.g1a_xi_squid_mm }} mm above it (SQUID), single-channel SNR equal to the peak field over the noise σ, and σ~OPM~ = η σ~SQUID~. The equal-SNR depth is the exact root of the preprint's Eq. 3. The peak field was checked against the full Sarvas field maximized over the sensor sphere (largest relative difference {{ F.g1a_eq1_vs_sarvas_rel_err_opm }}) and against MNE-Python's sphere model ({{ F.g1a_eq1_vs_mne_rel_err_opm }}).

The reimplementation reproduces the preprint. At η = {{ F.lit_eta_reference }} the exact equal-SNR depth is {{ F.g1a_deq_eta3_mm_3dp }} mm. The preprint prints {{ F.g1a_fig3_deq_printed_mm }} mm in its text and caption, and its figure draws {{ F.g1a_fig3_deq_drawn_mm }} mm; the difference is the preprint's rounding and grid, not a difference of model. A crossing exists only for η between {{ F.g1a_eta0_exact }} and {{ F.g1a_eta1_exact }} (printed {{ F.g1a_eta0_printed }} and {{ F.g1a_eta1_printed }}). Only η enters the equal-SNR depth: scaling both noises together leaves it at {{ F.g1a_deq_sigma_independent_mm }} mm.

**Transfer to the real head.** On the real head, the peak field of the best OPM over that of the best Neuromag magnetometer falls with depth as the sphere predicts once the sphere is given the real median standoffs, {{ F.g2_matched_scalp_dist_median_mm }} mm for the OPMs and {{ F.g2_squid_scalp_dist_median_mm }} mm for the magnetometers. For the dense array the ratio falls from {{ F.g2_leadfield_dense_vs_mag_10_15_ratio }} in the {{ F.g2_depth_bin_10_15_mm }} mm bin to {{ F.g2_leadfield_dense_vs_mag_55_60_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_55_60_mm }} mm, against {{ F.g2_leadfield_sphere_real_10_15_ratio }} and {{ F.g2_leadfield_sphere_real_55_60_ratio }} for the sphere.

The bridge between the two models uses the sphere's own metric: peak-channel SNR against the magnetometers, with sensor noise only. With it, the realistic equal-SNR depth at η = {{ F.lit_eta_reference }} is {{ F.g2_bridge_deq_matched_eta3_mm }} mm for the site-matched array and {{ F.g2_bridge_deq_dense_eta3_mm }} mm for the dense array, against {{ F.g2_bridge_deq_sphere_real_eta3_mm }} mm for the sphere with the real standoffs. For η from {{ F.g2_bridge_common_eta_min }} to {{ F.g2_bridge_common_eta_max }} the real arrays' depths lie within {{ F.g2_bridge_deq_arrays_minus_sphere_real_mm_range }} mm of the sphere's. Outside that window they differ. The OPMs are ahead at every depth for η ≤ {{ F.g2_bridge_dense_eta_opm_ahead_max }}, where the sphere still predicts a deep crossing ({{ F.g2_bridge_deq_sphere_real_eta2p25_mm }} mm at η = {{ F.g2_bridge_dense_eta_opm_ahead_max }}), because the real peak-field ratio levels off at depth ({{ F.g2_leadfield_dense_vs_mag_60_65_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_60_65_mm }} mm) while the sphere's keeps falling ({{ F.g2_leadfield_sphere_real_60_65_ratio }}). Neuromag's magnetometers are ahead at every depth for η ≥ {{ F.g2_bridge_matched_eta_squid_ahead_min }} (site-matched) or {{ F.g2_bridge_dense_eta_squid_ahead_min }} (dense). The sphere is therefore right about the geometry of magnetometers near its reference η ([@sfig-sphere]). Against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_grad_channels }} planar gradiometers the sensor-noise-only ratio barely changes with depth, so the sphere describes only the magnetometer part of the comparison. What it leaves open is η itself, which the explicit noise model of section C replaces.

**Head size in the sphere.** For head size, the preprint's newborn, {{ F.g3a_infant1yr_age_years }}-year, {{ F.g3a_child8yr_age_years }}-year and adult spheres were placed in a SQUID shell that follows each head at {{ F.lit_xi_squid_mm }} mm, as in the preprint, and, as a new contrast, all in the adult shell of radius {{ F.g3a_fixed_shell_radius }} mm. With the size-following shell the equal-SNR depth at η = {{ F.lit_eta_reference }} hardly moves from adult to newborn ({{ F.g3a_follow_eta3_deq_range }} mm), but the normalized depth rises from {{ F.g3a_adult_follow_eta3_norm_1dp }} to {{ F.g3a_newborn_follow_eta3_norm_1dp }}, because the brain and the extracerebral layer ({{ F.lit_table1_extracerebral_range_mm }} mm in the preprint's heads) shrink. With one adult shell for every head, size and fit are confounded: the OPM is then ahead at every depth of the newborn and {{ F.g3a_infant1yr_age_years }}-year brains up to η = {{ F.g3a_newborn_fixed_eta0_1dp }} and {{ F.g3a_infant1yr_fixed_eta0_1dp }}. The sphere's prediction for children is thus conditional on a shell that follows the head at a constant gap. The panels of this head-size benchmark are shown on [S4](pediatric.html), the repository's own report of the smaller heads.

## C. The noise model: details, validation and sensitivity analyses {#s-noise}

### C.1 Components and calibration {#s-noise-model}

Every array sees identical noise sources in one analysis band of {{ F.g2_band_range_hz }} Hz (zero-phase Butterworth filter of order {{ F.g2_filter_order }}; equivalent noise bandwidth {{ F.g2_enbw_hz }} Hz). The three components are sensor noise, the cortical background and the room field.

*Sensor noise* is white: {{ F.g2_squid_mag_asd }} fT/√Hz for the Neuromag magnetometers and {{ F.g2_squid_grad_asd }} fT/(cm √Hz) for the gradiometers, the typical values of the TRIUX datasheet (Elekta Neuromag, n.d.), and {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz for the OPMs in the primary analysis, swept from {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_range }} fT/√Hz. The OPM value is a declared parameter, not a measured device value. It is the middle of the sweep and within the range of sensor noise quoted by the clinical OPM studies, although the clinical studies state no band for these figures and the device specifications behind them begin at 1 Hz or above (Osborne et al., 2018; [S7](register.html)); the top of the sweep is close to the empty-room floor read from the preprint's recording (a reading of its Fig. 9 that assumes its unstated dB reference is 1 fT²/Hz). The comparisons are reported across the sweep.

*The cortical background* consists of {{ F.g2_n_background }} independent dipoles normal to the cortex on a {{ F.g2_bg_grid_mm }}-mm grid, weighted by cortical area. Like the targets, it omits the cortex within {{ F.g2_source_skull_clearance_mm }} mm of the inner skull ({{ F.g2_excluded_vertices_pct_1dp }} of the vertices), the sources nearest to every sensor; section C.6 adds that cortex back. One scale was fitted so that the median good gradiometer's in-band variance equals the measured brain noise of the sample recording: the in-band variance of the pre-stimulus windows of its {{ F.g2_measured_baseline_windows }} stimuli, which begin soon after the previous stimulus and so hold task-related rather than only resting activity, minus that of the empty-room recording, the channel marked bad excluded. Spatially correlated variants used correlation lengths of {{ F.g2_bg_corr_lengths_mm }} mm.

*The room field* is an external field of {{ F.g2_room_terms }} terms (uniform plus linear gradient) fitted to the empty-room recording and passed through each array's coil integration.

**The covariance in closed form.** Each array's oracle covariance is computed without sampling: C = Σ~s~ + s² L~bg~ A L~bg~^T^ + E Σ~e~ E^T^. Σ~s~ is diagonal, each channel's squared white-noise spectral density times the filter's equivalent noise bandwidth ({{ F.meth_enbw_hz }} Hz). L~bg~ holds the array's lead fields of the {{ F.meth_bg_n_sources }} independent, cortex-normal background dipoles, a greedy Poisson-disk subset of the usable white-surface vertices with {{ F.meth_bg_grid_mm }}-mm minimum spacing (seed {{ F.meth_seed_g2 }}). A = diag(a~i~), a~i~ being the area of source i's Voronoi cell on the cortex; the cells partition the {{ F.meth_bg_area_cm2 }} cm² of usable cortex ({{ F.meth_bg_mean_area_mm2 }} mm² on average). Source i's moment variance is s² a~i~, with s² = {{ F.meth_bg_var_per_mm2_nam2 }} nAm² per mm² (standard deviation {{ F.meth_bg_sd_per_sqrt_mm2_nam }} nAm × √(a~i~/mm²); {{ F.meth_bg_rms_sd_nam }} nAm root-mean-square over the sources), fitted once so that the good gradiometers' median modelled variance equals their median measured brain noise ({{ F.meth_bg_calibration_grad_ft_cm }} fT/cm RMS), and kept per unit area on every head. E holds the array's responses, through its own coil integration points, to {{ F.meth_env_terms }} external-field terms ({{ F.meth_env_uniform_terms }} uniform, {{ F.meth_env_gradient_terms }} linear-gradient, about {{ F.meth_env_r0_mm }} mm in the head frame), and Σ~e~ is their coefficient covariance, fitted to the band-filtered empty-room recording. Plug-in covariances are Ledoit–Wolf estimates from {{ F.meth_plugin10s_samples }} or {{ F.meth_plugin60s_samples }} independent samples, twice the time-bandwidth product of {{ F.meth_plugin_seconds }} s.

**Projection.** P = I − E(EᵀWE)⁻¹EᵀW, with W = Σ~s~⁻¹, is applied to topographies and covariance alike (Ps, PCPᵀ), by the same rule for every array. For Neuromag it acts on all {{ F.meth_squid_channels }} channels jointly, so its magnetometer and gradiometer comparisons after projection use subsets of jointly projected data. Retained ranks: {{ F.meth_rank_squid_proj }} of {{ F.meth_squid_channels }} ({{ F.meth_rank_squid_mag_proj }} of {{ F.meth_squid_mags }} magnetometers, {{ F.meth_rank_squid_grad_proj }} of {{ F.meth_squid_grads }} gradiometers), {{ F.meth_rank_dense_proj }} of {{ F.meth_dense_sites }} (dense) and {{ F.meth_rank_matched_proj }} of {{ F.meth_matched_sites }} (site-matched). Whitening drops eigenvalues of the correlation-normalized covariance below {{ F.meth_whiten_tol }} of the largest. The projection costs Neuromag {{ F.g2_proj_cost_squid_pct_2sf }} of its detectability, the dense array {{ F.g2_proj_cost_dense_pct }} and the site-matched array {{ F.g2_proj_cost_matched_pct }}, relative to the room-field condition without projection.

**OPM sensitive axes.** Each OPM's axis is the normal of the BEM head surface (placed on the MRI scalp, normals from its own triangles) averaged within {{ F.meth_opm_axis_radius_mm }} mm of the site. It deviates from a plane fitted to the dense MRI scalp by a median {{ F.meth_opm_axis_dev_median_deg }}° ({{ F.meth_opm_axis_dev_pctl }} percentile {{ F.meth_opm_axis_dev_p95_matched_deg }}° site-matched, {{ F.meth_opm_axis_dev_p95_dense_deg }}° dense). An earlier construction took the adult's stored outer-skin normals, which lie within {{ F.meth_opm_axis_radial_deg }}° of the radial direction. ### C.2 What is fitted, calibrated, checked and predicted {#s-noise-fitted}

Four kinds of agreement must be kept apart ([@sfig-noise-budget]). For the magnetometers the room field was *fitted* to the empty-room recording: with the manufacturer's sensor noise it reproduces their empty-room level ({{ F.g2_model_empty_room_mag_ft }} against {{ F.g2_measured_empty_room_mag_ft }} fT measured) and explains {{ F.g2_room_explained_mag_pct }} of their empty-room variance. For the gradiometers the room field explains only {{ F.g2_room_explained_grad_pct }} of that variance, so their empty-room agreement ({{ F.g2_model_empty_room_grad_ftcm }} against {{ F.g2_measured_empty_room_grad_ftcm }} fT/cm measured) is an independent *check* of the model's sensor-noise term, the datasheet's typical value, against this one recording, whose level it slightly exceeds. The gradiometer brain noise is *calibrated*: {{ F.g2_model_brain_grad_ftcm }} fT/cm, equal to the measurement by construction. The magnetometer brain noise is the one independent *prediction* of the background at the level of a median, and it falls short: {{ F.g2_model_brain_mag_ft }} fT modelled against {{ F.g2_measured_brain_mag_ft }} fT measured ({{ F.g2_brain_mag_model_over_measured_ratio }} times). The measured value is the excess of the task baseline over the empty room, which also holds non-cortical physiological fields that the model omits by design, so the shortfall is not purely an error of the cortical background; section C.4 separates the heart's part. Nothing about the OPM noise is measured: its sensor noise is declared, and its brain and room noise are the Neuromag-calibrated model seen through the OPM arrays' own lead fields.

Two published simulation studies check shape and level ([S2](benchmarks.html)). The depth-orientation maps of Hunold et al. (2016), adapted to this anatomy, correlate with the published dipole maps at r = {{ F.g1b_dipole_r_range }} but reach only {{ F.g1b_dipole_level_range }} times their level (patch maps: r = {{ F.g1b_patch_r_range }}, {{ F.g1b_patch_level_range }} times), even with a background scale fitted to the paper's example traces. The calibration rule of Goldenholz et al. (2009) gives a noise-source amplitude within their published range once normalized to their number of sources ({{ F.g1c_source_sd_4000_nam }} against {{ F.g1c_paper_source_sd_nam_range }} nAm; {{ F.g1c_source_sd_nam }} nAm on our grid). Each adaptation keeps its paper's design rather than this study's model: Hunold et al.'s {{ F.g1b_dipole_peak_nam }}-nAm spike-wave sources over a background of randomly placed cortical dipoles, conductivities of {{ F.g1b_conductivity }} S/m and their peak-to-peak SNR, and Goldenholz et al.'s {{ F.g1c_focal_nam }}-nAm dipoles, channel-averaged power SNR and a cortical background calibrated on recorded noise. Both adaptations already show the dependence on metric that the main text examines. With brain noise only, a site-matched OPM array's best single channel in our adaptation of Hunold's dipole maps reaches only {{ F.g1b_opm_vs_grad_rows_range }} times the SNR of the planar gradiometers in every depth row, and {{ F.g1b_opm_vs_mag_rows_range }} times that of the magnetometers, falling with depth, whereas with Goldenholz's channel-averaged SNR the OPM is within about 0.1 dB of both (point estimates: {{ F.g1c_opm_minus_grad_focal_brain_only_db }} dB against the gradiometers and {{ F.g1c_opm_minus_mag_focal_brain_only_db }} dB against the magnetometers).

**The noise budget.** In this model brain noise dominates both kinds of magnetometer channel: it is {{ F.g2_noise_share_dense_brain_pct }} of a dense-array OPM channel's in-band variance (room field {{ F.g2_noise_share_dense_room_pct }}, sensor noise {{ F.g2_noise_share_dense_sensor_pct }}) and {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_brain_pct }} of a Neuromag magnetometer's (room field {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_room_pct }}, sensor noise {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_sensor_pct }}), whereas sensor noise is {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_grad_sensor_pct }} of a gradiometer's. In RMS terms a dense-array channel carries {{ F.g2_noise_dense_brain }} fT of brain noise, {{ F.g2_noise_dense_room }} fT of room field and {{ F.g2_noise_dense_intrinsic }} fT of sensor noise in the band, against {{ F.g2_noise_squid_mag_brain }}, {{ F.g2_noise_squid_mag_room }} and {{ F.g2_noise_squid_mag_intrinsic }} fT at the magnetometers. The implied single-channel noise ratio OPM/magnetometer, the analogue of η, is about {{ F.g2_noise_rms_dense_vs_mag_noroom_ratio_1dp }} in the {{ F.g2_band_range_hz }} Hz band with sensor plus brain noise, the primary condition ({{ F.g2_noise_rms_dense_vs_mag_ratio_1dp }} with the room field added), against about {{ F.lit_sef_noise_ratio_closest }} in the preprint's recording over a much wider band ({{ F.lit_sef_band_range_hz }} Hz). Approximate readings of the preprint's spectra, not stored results, locate the difference: where brain activity dominates, the OPM/SQUID ratio of the spectral peaks is about {{ F.lit_sef_midband_noise_ratio }}, somewhat below the model's {{ F.g2_noise_rms_dense_vs_mag_noroom_ratio_1dp }}, whereas the measured OPM has an empty-room floor of about {{ F.lit_sef_opm_floor_asd_range }} fT/√Hz and a distance-independent low-frequency component of about {{ F.lit_sef_opm_lowfreq_peak_asd_range }} fT/√Hz at its peak. ### C.3 Why the ratio falls with depth, and the other adult conditions {#s-noise-depth}

Against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels the fall of the dense ratio with depth is already present with sensor noise alone, from {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_combined_int_10_15_ratio }} to {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_combined_int_bins_min }}, because Neuromag's sensor-noise-only detectability rests on its magnetometers, and the peak-field ratio OPM/magnetometer falls from {{ F.g2_leadfield_dense_vs_mag_10_15_ratio }} in the shallowest bin to {{ F.g2_leadfield_dense_vs_mag_bins_min }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_55_60_mm }} mm; the detectability ratio falls with it, though less steeply, since the whole-array measure draws on the field at many channels. Against the {{ F.g2_squid_grad_channels }} gradiometers alone the sensor-noise-only ratio barely changes with depth ({{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_grad_int_10_15_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_10_15_mm }} mm, {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_grad_int_20_25_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_20_25_mm }} mm and {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_grad_int_60_65_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_60_65_mm }} mm), yet with brain noise it falls from {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_grad_ib_10_15_ratio }} to {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_grad_ib_35_40_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_35_40_mm }} mm: there the depth dependence comes from brain noise, which costs every array most for deep sources. Against all {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels, adding brain noise multiplies the dense array's ratio by a nearly constant factor in every depth bin ({{ F.g2_brainmult_dense_vs_combined_range }}), whereas for the site-matched array the factor rises with depth ({{ F.g2_brainmult_matched_vs_combined_range }}) and partly offsets the geometric fall. The size of the factor follows the per-channel noise budget rather than better whitening by the denser array: brain noise multiplies the in-band noise of a Neuromag magnetometer about {{ F.g2_noise_inflation_squid_mag_1dp }}-fold over its sensor floor but that of an OPM only about {{ F.g2_noise_inflation_dense_1dp }}-fold (a ratio of {{ F.g2_noise_inflation_mag_over_dense_ratio }}), and the sparser site-matched array has the larger factor.

**Where the dense array is not ahead.** With sensor plus brain noise the dense array is behind Neuromag at {{ F.rev_g2_notahead_n }} of the {{ F.g2_n_targets }} targets. They lie on the {{ F.rev_g2_notahead_regions }}, {{ F.rev_g2_notahead_depth_mm_range }} mm below the scalp, with ratios of {{ F.rev_g2_notahead_ratio_range }} (`results/g2/g2_targets_metrics.csv` gives every target's detectabilities at full precision, with an explicit flag where the OPM is ahead). Pooled over both hemispheres, {{ F.reg_dk_dense_vs_combined_ib_below_parahippocampal_count }} atlas parcels have a smaller dense lead than the parahippocampal cortex ({{ F.reg_parahippocampal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}): posterior cingulate ({{ F.reg_dk_posteriorcingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), precuneus ({{ F.reg_dk_precuneus_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), caudal anterior cingulate ({{ F.reg_dk_caudalanteriorcingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), transverse temporal ({{ F.reg_dk_transversetemporal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), lingual ({{ F.reg_dk_lingual_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), pericalcarine ({{ F.reg_dk_pericalcarine_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), paracentral ({{ F.reg_dk_paracentral_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), rostral anterior cingulate ({{ F.reg_dk_rostralanteriorcingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), isthmus cingulate ({{ F.reg_dk_isthmuscingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), insula ({{ F.reg_dk_insula_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), medial orbitofrontal ({{ F.reg_dk_medialorbitofrontal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}) and banks of the superior temporal sulcus ({{ F.reg_dk_bankssts_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}).

**Plug-in covariance.** A covariance estimated from {{ F.g2_plugin10s_samples }} samples costs Neuromag more than the OPMs ({{ F.g2_plugin10s_loss_squid_combined_ib_pct }} against {{ F.g2_plugin10s_loss_dense_ib_pct }} for the dense array and {{ F.g2_plugin10s_loss_matched_ib_pct }} for the site-matched array, of the oracle detectability), raising the dense ratio to {{ F.g2_plugin10s_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} and the site-matched ratio to {{ F.g2_plugin10s_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}. ### C.4 The model against the measured Neuromag covariance {#s-noise-covcheck}

**Data and comparison.** The measured brain noise is the covariance of the sample recording's {{ F.g2_measured_baseline_windows }} pre-stimulus windows in the {{ F.rev_cov_band_hz }} Hz band minus that of the empty-room recording, with the bad channel ({{ F.rev_cov_bad_channels }}) left out and no signal-space projection, as in the adult comparison; {{ F.rev_cov_det_n_channels_combined }} channels remain ({{ F.rev_cov_det_n_channels_mag }} magnetometers, {{ F.rev_cov_det_n_channels_grad }} gradiometers). Two quantities of the model are fitted to this dataset: the one scalar of the cortical background (the median good-gradiometer brain variance) and the room field's {{ F.meth_env_terms }} × {{ F.meth_env_terms }} coefficient covariance (the empty room). Everything else is compared as an independent prediction: the magnetometers' brain-noise amplitude and channel pattern, the gradiometer level at held-out sites, the spatial correlations against inter-sensor distance, the eigenvalue spectra and dominant subspaces, the sub-band spectra, and Neuromag's detectability with the measured covariance. Where a sample covariance is compared with an exact one, the comparison is calibrated on surrogates: an exact model "measured" the same way (phase-randomized records recoloured to the model, with the same number of samples) gives the range that an exact model would show, so a value outside that range is a real departure of the measured noise from the model.

**Amplitude and held-out sites.** The model predicts the magnetometers' median brain-noise amplitude at {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_amp_ratio }} of the measured; an exact model would give {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_amp_null_range }}. For the gradiometers, the scale fitted on half the sites predicts the other half's median amplitude to {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_random_grad_amp_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_random_grad_amp_ci }} over {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_n_splits }} random halves, but to {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_inf_to_sup_grad_amp_ratio }} when fitted on the inferior half and predicting the superior half, and {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_sup_to_inf_grad_amp_ratio }} the other way round: the model's spatial distribution of brain noise is too flat from bottom to top.

**Channel pattern.** The per-channel pattern is poorly predicted. The Pearson correlation of the log variances, model against measured, is {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_log_var_r }} for the magnetometers and {{ F.rev_cov_brain_grad_log_var_r }} for the gradiometers, against a split-half reliability of the measurement of {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_log_var_r_split_half }} and {{ F.rev_cov_brain_grad_log_var_r_split_half }}. The per-channel model/measured variance spans {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_var_ratio_p5_p95_range }} (magnetometers, 5th to 95th percentile) and {{ F.rev_cov_brain_grad_var_ratio_p5_p95_range }} (gradiometers), where an exact model would give about {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_var_ratio_null_p5_p95_range }}. The correlation pattern between channels agrees better: the measured and modelled correlation matrices correlate at r = {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_corr_pattern_r }} (magnetometers) and {{ F.rev_cov_brain_grad_corr_pattern_r }} (gradiometers).

**Spatial structure.** ([@sfig-covariance-structure]) Against inter-sensor distance, the modelled magnetometer correlations follow the measured ones at {{ F.wr_cov_dist_d25_50_label }} mm ({{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d25_50_model_r }} against {{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d25_50_measured_r }}) and fall faster beyond ({{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d50_75_model_r }} against {{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d50_75_measured_r }} at {{ F.wr_cov_dist_d50_75_label }} mm): at {{ F.wr_cov_dist_d100_125_label }} mm the model gives {{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d100_125_model_r }} against {{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d100_125_measured_r }} measured, and at {{ F.wr_cov_dist_d200_225_label }} mm {{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d200_225_model_r }} against {{ F.rev_cov_dist_mag_d200_225_measured_r }}. Spatially correlated backgrounds (correlation lengths of {{ F.g2_bg_corr_lengths_mm }} mm) do not change this, because the measured long-range correlation is a far-field pattern, not a cortical one. The gradiometers' median signed correlations agree at every distance ({{ F.rev_cov_dist_grad_d25_50_model_r }} against {{ F.rev_cov_dist_grad_d25_50_measured_r }} at {{ F.wr_cov_dist_d25_50_label }} mm). The measured magnetometer brain noise is more concentrated than the model's: its leading five components hold {{ F.rev_cov_eig_mag_measured_top5_pct }} of the variance against {{ F.rev_cov_eig_mag_model_top5_pct }} in the model (participation ratio {{ F.rev_cov_eig_mag_measured_participation }} against {{ F.rev_cov_eig_mag_model_participation }}), whereas the gradiometer spectra agree ({{ F.rev_cov_eig_grad_measured_top5_pct }} against {{ F.rev_cov_eig_grad_model_top5_pct }}). The magnetometers' excess over the model is concentrated: its leading three components hold {{ F.rev_cov_excess_brain_mag_top3_pct }} of it, and {{ F.rev_cov_excess_brain_mag_lead1_external_pct }} of the leading component lies in the {{ F.meth_env_terms }}-dimensional far-field subspace of the room-field terms (a random direction: {{ F.rev_cov_excess_brain_mag_external_random_pct }}).

**The heart.** A cardiac-locked average over {{ F.rev_cov_heart_n_beats }} beats (heart rate {{ F.rev_cov_heart_rate_bpm }} per minute) adds {{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_rms_ft }} fT RMS to the median magnetometer's baseline windows: {{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_share_brain_pct }} of the measured magnetometer brain variance and {{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_share_shortfall_pct }} of the model's shortfall. Its leading component lies {{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_leading_external_pct }} in the far-field subspace. With the heart's field removed the magnetometer prediction is {{ F.rev_cov_noheart_mag_amp_ratio }} of the measured amplitude, and the correlation of the log variances rises to {{ F.rev_cov_noheart_mag_log_var_r }} (magnetometers) and {{ F.rev_cov_noheart_grad_log_var_r }} (gradiometers). The heart therefore explains about half of the shortfall and much of the pattern error; the rest is concentrated in far-field components, consistent with distant or non-cortical sources, or with cortical activity correlated over longer distances than the {{ F.g2_bg_corr_lengths_mm }} mm tested.

### C.5 Scenarios A to E: what the check implies for the OPM {#s-noise-scenarios}

The OPM's covariance cannot be measured here. The implied OPM/Neuromag ratio with sensor, brain and room noise (like for like with the measured covariance, which holds the room field; Neuromag's {{ F.rev_cov_det_n_channels_combined }} good channels) was therefore computed under five stated assumptions about the OPM's real noise:

- *Scenario A*, the model's error common to both systems: the OPM's real covariance changes its detectability by the same factor, target by target, as the measured covariance changes Neuromag's. The ratio is the model's own.
- *Scenario B*, both systems' cortical backgrounds scaled by the measured magnetometer variance excess k = {{ F.rev_cov_k_ratio }}, the model's structure kept.
- *Scenario C*, the most pessimistic: Neuromag with its measured covariance, the OPM bearing the whole magnetometer variance excess as cortical noise and none of its measured structure.
- *Scenario D*, Neuromag with its measured covariance, the OPM exactly as modelled (the model's error is Neuromag-specific).
- *Scenario E*, Neuromag with its measured empty room and the modelled brain noise, the OPM as modelled (the sensor and room part of Neuromag's noise alone).

[@stab-scenarios] gives the ratios. Scenarios A, B and E leave the primary model's ratios nearly unchanged. Scenario D takes the dense array to {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ci_3dp }}, no established advantage, and the site-matched array below 1; by depth it leaves the dense array's median at {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_depth_10_15_ratio }} and {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_depth_15_20_ratio }} in the {{ F.g2_depth_bin_10_15_mm }} and {{ F.g2_depth_bin_15_20_mm }} mm bins and takes it below 1 from the {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_depth_first_below1_label }} mm bin (point estimates over the per-target values, room field included). Scenario C puts both arrays well below 1. Which scenario holds depends on how the OPM's real noise departs from the model, which no measurement here constrains; the noise model is validated in part for Neuromag, not for the OPM arrays.

### C.6 Sensitivity analyses {#s-noise-sensitivity}

Each analysis changes one element of the adult model and recalibrates the cortical background on the measured gradiometer brain noise, as the adult comparison does; the joint runs change them together. Intervals are {{ F.meth_boot_noise_sens }} parcel-bootstrap resamples of one anatomy. The spike simulations were not repeated under these alternatives.

**Near-skull cortex.** The primary model omits the cortex within {{ F.rev_ns_near_rule_mm }} mm of the inner skull: {{ F.rev_ns_near_n_vertices }} white-surface vertices, {{ F.rev_ns_near_share_valid_pct }} of the valid ones and {{ F.rev_ns_near_area_cm2 }} cm² of cortex ({{ F.rev_ns_near_area_pct }}). The lead fields of sources closer than about {{ F.rev_ns_joint_floor_mm }} mm to the inner-skull mesh are not converged, so the primary variants add the cortex from {{ F.rev_ns_joint_floor_mm }} mm outward: as additional background noise, as additional targets ({{ F.rev_ns_near_f2mm_n_targets }} of the {{ F.rev_ns_near_n_targets }} near-skull targets), or both. Removing near-skull sources from the background and from the targets have opposite effects, so the exclusion is not conservative by construction. The dense ratio is {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_bg_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_bg_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} with the near-skull cortex as noise, {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_targets_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_targets_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} with it as targets and {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_both_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_both_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} with both (site-matched {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_bg_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }}, {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_targets_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} and {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_both_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }}), against {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} in the primary model. ## D. Anatomies and the children's MRI check {#s-anatomies}

### D.1 The adult and the three classes of smaller head {#s-anatomies-classes}

The adult is the MNE sample subject (Gramfort et al., 2013), with FreeSurfer surfaces and atlas labels (Fischl, 2012; Desikan et al., 2006) and a three-layer boundary-element model whose outer surface lies on the MRI scalp. Its usable cortex is {{ F.g3b_adult_cortex_area_cm2 }} cm². The smaller heads belong to three classes that are not independent subjects.

*Scaled adults.* The {{ F.wr_n_scaled_words }} scaled adults are copies of the adult scaled by one factor (size only): the school-age size control at {{ F.g3b_cfg_school_scale }} times the adult, and the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_years }}-year size control at the head circumference of the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}-month template. They keep the adult's vertices, so Δ is vertex-wise.

*Infant templates.* The {{ F.wr_n_templates_words }} infant templates at {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }} and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_age_months }} months (O'Reilly et al., 2021) average many MRIs of the Neurodevelopmental MRI Database (Richards et al., 2016) and have smooth cortices: their usable cortex is {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_cortex_area_cm2 }}, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_cortex_area_cm2 }} and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_cortex_area_cm2 }} cm², {{ F.g3b_templates_cortex_area_pct_of_adult_range }} of the adult's, which lowers their total background power and their patch cancellation. They were used at their native size with their own three-layer BEMs, oct-6 source spaces, atlas labels and fiducials.

*School-aged children.* The {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} individual children A, B and C ({{ F.g3b_children_age_range }} years; subjects {{ F.rev_qc_child_a_subject }}, {{ F.rev_qc_child_b_subject }} and {{ F.rev_qc_child_c_subject }}) are typically developing children of the OpenNeuro dataset ds005234 (Fadeev et al., 2024, 2025; de-identified, data licence CC0), with their own MRIs processed by the dataset's authors. Their white and sphere surfaces, atlas annotations and dense MRI scalp were used as distributed; they share each child's volume information. The dataset's boundary-element surfaces were taken from the file tree as it is distributed, in which each subject's watershed surfaces sit in the preceding subject's folder; the files were matched to each child by the volume information they share with the child's own surfaces, and the storage objects behind them carry the name of the subject they belong to. These watershed surfaces put the inner skull implausibly close to the scalp, a median {{ F.g3b_children_watershed_inner_skull_depth_p50_range }} mm below it over the upper head, with an outer skull crossing it. The children's skull was therefore modelled: the inner skull was moved inward to {{ F.g3b_cfg_child_skull_depth }} mm below the scalp wherever the dataset's lay shallower, with no vertex within {{ F.g3b_cfg_child_skull_min_cortex }} mm of a white-surface vertex (the surface between vertices comes within {{ F.g3b_children_white_to_inner_skull_closest_range }} mm of the white surface; no white vertex lies outside it), and the outer skull was put about halfway between it and the scalp. Over the upper head the modelled inner skull lies a median {{ F.g3b_children_modelled_inner_skull_depth_p50_range }} mm below the scalp. On the adult, whose segmented skull is known, the same procedure applied to a degraded inner skull changes the primary ratio by at most {{ F.g3b_bemval_max_ratio_change }} over the modelled skull depths. The head surface of the forward model is the watershed outer skin put on the MRI scalp, as for every head.

No fiducials come with the dataset. The children's fiducials, which define the head frame and so the centred placement, the origin of every scaled helmet and the OPM coverage region, were transferred from the adult by a similarity fit of the cortices and moved to the nearest scalp vertex. On the templates, which have their own fiducials, this transfer reproduces them to within {{ F.g3b_templates_fid_transfer_range }} mm and their head frames to within {{ F.g3b_templates_fid_transfer_rotation_range }}°; the largest error, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_fid_transfer_nasion }} mm, is at the {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }}-month template's nasion, which lies farthest from the cortex. Frame rotations of this size (total angles, their axis not resolved) are of the order of the placement family's rotations (pitch ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_pitch }}°, yaw ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_yaw }}°, roll ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_roll }}°; section E.1), which indicates rather than bounds their effect on the children's placements; the transferred fiducials were not checked on the children's own MRIs. Fiducials from MNI coordinates were not used: on the adult they land {{ F.g3b_adult_mni_fid_range }} mm from the digitized ones.

*Conductivities.* Every head uses the adult's conductivities ({{ F.meth_conductivity }} S/m). With a single-compartment BEM ({{ F.meth_bem1_conductivity }} S/m inside the inner skull), which needs no skull or scalp conductivity, the difference of medians from the adult is {{ F.g3b_smaller_bem1_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_range }} dB, against {{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_range }} dB with three layers ([S4](pediatric.html)); across all smaller heads that difference changes by {{ F.meth_bem1_change_db_range }} dB.

*Thin skull of the youngest template.* As distributed, the {{ F.meth_infant12mo_age_months }}-month template's skull is {{ F.meth_infant12mo_skull_min_mm }} mm thick at its thinnest ({{ F.meth_infant12mo_thin_vertices }} of {{ F.meth_infant12mo_inner_skull_vertices }} inner-skull vertices lie within {{ F.meth_infant12mo_thin_within_mm }} mm of the outer skull). The single-compartment BEM has no skull and is unaffected. With it the template's D is {{ F.meth_infant12mo_d_bem1_db }} dB against {{ F.meth_infant12mo_d_db }} dB with three layers, and its difference from the adult {{ F.meth_infant12mo_d_minus_adult_bem1_db }} against {{ F.meth_infant12mo_d_minus_adult_db }} dB, so the thin skull does not carry the template's result.

### D.2 Regions {#s-anatomies-regions}

Regions are the lobes of the Desikan–Killiany atlas (Desikan et al., 2006) as grouped by the study: frontal (superior, rostral middle and caudal middle frontal, pars opercularis, triangularis and orbitalis, lateral and medial orbitofrontal, precentral, paracentral and frontal pole); parietal (superior and inferior parietal, supramarginal, postcentral and precuneus); temporal (superior, middle and inferior temporal, banks of the superior temporal sulcus, fusiform, transverse temporal, entorhinal, temporal pole and parahippocampal); occipital (lateral occipital, lingual, cuneus and pericalcarine); cingulate (rostral and caudal anterior, posterior and isthmus); and insula. The medial wall belongs to none. Single parcels that the epilepsy literature emphasizes are reported as well: precentral, superior temporal ('lateral temporal' in the figures) and parahippocampal, with a mesial temporal group of the parahippocampal and entorhinal parcels; 'mesial temporal' always means this group. Both hemispheres are pooled. All sources lie on the cortical surface; hippocampus and amygdala are not modelled, so the parahippocampal and entorhinal cortex, {{ F.reg_mesial_parcels_depth_range }} mm below the scalp in the adult (median over targets), are the nearest proxies for mesial temporal generators. The regions were chosen from the literature, and rankings among them are exploratory.

### D.3 The MRI quality check of the school-aged children and the infant templates {#s-anatomies-qc}

**Why it was needed.** The children's white surfaces come within {{ F.g3b_children_white_to_scalp_closest_range }} mm of the scalp surface used for sensor placement and source depths: child B has {{ F.g3b_child_b_shallow_targets_lt10mm }} cortical targets shallower than {{ F.g3b_shallow_target_limit_mm }} mm, the adult none. Child B's head is the size of the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}-month template's (head circumference {{ F.g3b_child_b_ofc_cm }} against {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_ofc_cm }} cm; cap volume {{ F.g3b_child_b_cap_volume_cm3 }} against {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_cap_volume_cm3 }} cm³) while its cortex is much larger ({{ F.g3b_child_b_cortex_area_cm2 }} against {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_cortex_area_cm2 }} cm²). Both pointed to a scalp surface lying inside the true scalp, or misregistered relative to the white surface. The check was run on the three children with the adult and the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}-month template as references, using the surfaces exactly as the forward models use them, and then in the same way on the {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }}- and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_age_months }}-month templates (`results/g3b_templates_qc/children_qc.json`).

**What was measured.** Along the outward normal of every vertex of the dense scalp, the T1 intensity was sampled from {{ F.rev_qc_profile_range_mm }} mm along the normal in {{ F.rev_qc_profile_step_mm }}-mm steps. The MRI head boundary is where the profile falls through the volume's air/tissue threshold (Otsu's method; the primary edge), with the half-maximum edge and the steepest gradient as secondary definitions. The signed offset of this boundary from the scalp used, positive outward, was summarized over the cap (the head above the plane of the fiducials) by its median, quartiles and 5th to 95th percentiles, and decomposed into a uniform offset, a translation and a rotation. The frames were checked by comparing the volume geometry of every surface with the T1 header. The white surface's registration to the T1 was checked by the white/grey contrast across it: the rigid shift that maximizes the contrast was searched coarsely and finely, and the contrast gain of the best shift is reported. The head circumference was measured on the MRI head boundary and on the scalp used. Targets below {{ F.rev_qc_near_mm }} mm were counted against both surfaces.

**Verdict rules.** A head is *misregistered* if a surface's header differs from the T1 by more than the header tolerance, or if the white surface's realigning shift, edge translation or rotation displacement exceeds {{ F.rev_qc_tolerance_mm }} mm. It is *inside* or *outside* if the cap median of the boundary's offset exceeds or falls short of the adult's by more than {{ F.rev_qc_tolerance_mm }} mm (one voxel) at both the Otsu and the half-maximum edge. Otherwise it is *usable*.

**Tests on the adult.** A declared inward displacement of the adult's scalp by {{ F.rev_qc_test_inward_declared_mm }} mm was recovered as {{ F.rev_qc_test_inward_recovered_mm }} mm, and a {{ F.rev_qc_test_translation_declared_mm }}-mm translation as {{ F.rev_qc_test_translation_recovered_mm }} mm ({{ F.rev_qc_test_translation_explained_pct }} of the offset pattern explained by a translation). ## E. Helmet fit: all constructions {#s-helmets}

### E.1 The fixed adult helmet and its placements {#s-helmets-fixed}

The same Neuromag sensors and noise were used for every head. The *centred* placement puts each head's own head frame where the adult's lies in the sample recording (the recording's device-to-head transform), so that the ear line stays where the adult's was. The primary placement is *top contact*: the head is raised from the centred pose until the nearest magnetometer coil centre is {{ F.g3b_cfg_clearance }} mm from the scalp (the {{ F.g3b_cfg_dewar_spacing }}-mm Dewar spacing plus a margin). This is a best-fit assumption rather than documented practice: a child in an adult helmet may be placed centrally and repositioned towards the expected source (Gaetz et al., 2008), and a small child's shoulders can stop the head short of the top (Tesan et al., 2010), so top contact favours Neuromag; the other placements bound this choice. A family of {{ F.g3b_n_family_placements }} placements chosen from the geometry alone tests placement sensitivity ({{ F.g3b_child_c_n_family_feasible }} for child C, for whom one lateral shift was infeasible): top contact, back contact, shifts of ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_translation }} mm left–right and front–back, and rotations of ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_pitch }}° pitch, ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_roll }}° roll and ±{{ F.g3b_cfg_yaw }}° yaw, each raised to the same contact. Further placements are the centred pose itself, a *laterally centred* pose raised to contact (the head moved sideways to the helmet's midline before the raise), and *true contact* at the Dewar spacing.

Over its {{ F.g3b_n_family_placements }} source-blind placements the adult's D spans {{ F.g3b_adult_family_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_range }} dB (a spread of {{ F.g3b_adult_family_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_span }} dB), with top contact at rank {{ F.g3b_adult_top_rank_in_family }} from the lowest. In the fixed helmet the smaller heads' Δ is {{ F.g3b_smaller_centred_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB at the centred pose, {{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB at top contact, {{ F.g3b_smaller_xcentred_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB laterally centred at contact and {{ F.g3b_smaller_top18mm_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB at true contact, each against the adult placed by the same rule. **OPM arrays and noise.** The OPM arrays were refitted to each head with the adult's rules: {{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_sites_range }} dense and {{ F.g3b_smaller_matched_sites_range }} site-matched sites on the smaller heads, at a median standoff of {{ F.g3b_all_dense_standoff_2dp_range }} mm above every head's own MRI scalp. They are the same in every helmet. Sensor noise, room field and band were unchanged, and the cortical background variance per unit cortical area was calibrated once on the adult, so a smaller cortex carries less total background (multiplied by {{ F.g3b_cfg_bg_factor_low }} and by {{ F.g3b_cfg_bg_factor_high }} as a sensitivity analysis).

**Four candidate routes.** In the realistic heads, fit and head size can act on D through at least four routes: a wider SQUID gap and a shallower cortex would raise it, while fewer OPM sites and, in the school-aged children, a near-adult cortex close to the OPMs would lower it. The constructions below vary these together in different combinations; none isolates one route (main text, Section 3.5), and the last route rests on the children's unverified near-scalp anatomy (section D.3).

### E.2 The helmet scaled with the head {#s-helmets-scaled}

Every coil centre is scaled about the head origin by the child-to-adult ratio of head circumferences, keeping coil size, orientation and noise, with the factor raised in steps until no coil is within {{ F.g3b_cfg_dewar_spacing }} mm of the scalp. The helmet is thus scaled in proportion to the head, gaps included, unlike the sphere's constant gap. It was computed about the centred head and, as a variant added after the first pediatric results to remove the templates' lateral offset, about the laterally centred head. It is a geometric construction that asks what the adult's fit would give at a child's size, not a model of a pediatric SQUID device. In the main text it is a secondary construction, because it also brings the sensors closer than the adult's own fit (below).

In the helmet scaled about the laterally centred head, Δ is {{ F.g3b_smaller_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB against the adult in its own scaled helmet. The upper interval bounds reach {{ F.g3b_smaller_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci_hi_max }} dB, child C's (parcel-bootstrap intervals, which exclude placement and construction uncertainty). Only child C, whose scaled helmet keeps a median gap of {{ F.g3b_child_c_cfx_gap }} mm against the adult's {{ F.g3b_adult_cfx_gap }} mm, is not negative ({{ F.g3b_child_c_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.g3b_child_c_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} dB, an interval that also contains its fixed-helmet Δ). With the room field projected out, the difference of medians from the adult in its own scaled helmet is {{ F.g3b_smaller_cfx_dense_vs_combined_proj_d_minus_adult_cfx_range }} dB ({{ F.g3b_smaller_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_cfx_range }} dB with sensor plus brain noise). Scaled about the centred head instead, the helmet leaves residual gains in the heads that sit off-centre, the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}- and {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }}-month templates and child C ({{ F.g3b_infant2yr_cf_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }}, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_cf_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} and {{ F.g3b_child_c_cf_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} dB; Δ {{ F.g3b_smaller_cf_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB overall). The OPM's absolute advantage remains in the scaled helmet (D {{ F.g3b_smaller_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_range }} dB in the smaller heads): the additional child–adult contrast is no longer established.

Three checks qualify this result. First, part of the drop comes from the reference: against the adult at top contact rather than in its own scaled helmet, the difference of medians is {{ F.g3b_smaller_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_top_range }} dB (child C {{ F.g3b_child_c_cfx_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_top }} dB, above its fixed-helmet Δ). Second, scaling the helmet also scales the gap, which shrinks below the adult's {{ F.g3b_adult_cfx_gap }} mm in {{ F.g3b_cfx_gap_below_adult_count }} of the {{ F.g4_n_smaller_heads }} smaller heads ({{ F.g3b_cfx_gap_below_adult_range }} mm). This helmet therefore fits most smaller heads more tightly than the adult's helmet fits the adult, which favours Neuromag; the helmet fitted at the adult's gap (section E.3) avoids this confound. Third, the smaller heads carry fewer OPM sites ({{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_sites_range }} against {{ F.g3b_adult_dense_sites }}): the adult's array subsampled to those counts loses {{ F.g3b_smaller_site_penalty_vs_combined_range }} dB, so at an equal OPM site count Δ in the fixed helmet would be {{ F.g3b_smaller_eqcount_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB.

**The within-head contrast and the interaction.** The within-head contrast says what Neuromag loses to the fixed helmet's fit, gap and placement together, in a given head: {{ F.rev_cgap_scaled_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib_range }} dB in the scaled adults, {{ F.rev_cgap_templates_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib_range }} dB in the templates and {{ F.rev_cgap_children_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib_range }} dB in the children; the adult's own contrast is {{ F.rev_cgap_adult_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib }} dB, because its laterally centred pose in its own helmet is looser than top contact. The interaction, the smaller head's contrast minus the adult's, is the extra that the fixed helmet gives a smaller head beyond what it gives the adult: {{ F.rev_cgap_scaled_fitted_combined_ib_interaction_range }} dB in the scaled adults, {{ F.rev_cgap_templates_fitted_combined_ib_interaction_range }} dB in the templates and {{ F.rev_cgap_children_fitted_combined_ib_interaction_range }} dB in the children. The area-weighted medians of these quantities are not additive, so Δ(fixed) − Δ(fitted) − interaction leaves a residual, given in [@stab-helmet-results]; the within-head and interaction estimates are therefore reported in their own right rather than derived from the Δ values.

### E.4 Depth matching {#s-helmets-depth}

Depth is the route of head size that the sphere describes. The templates' and children's cortex is shallower than the adult's: area-weighted median depths of {{ F.g3b_templates_depth_wmedian_range }} mm (templates) and {{ F.g3b_children_depth_wmedian_range }} mm (children) against {{ F.g3b_adult_depth_wmedian }} mm, with {{ F.g3b_templates_area_share_10_20mm_pct_range }} and {{ F.g3b_children_area_share_10_20mm_pct_range }} of the cortical area {{ F.g3b_depth_span_10_20_mm }} mm below the scalp against {{ F.g3b_adult_area_share_10_20mm_pct }} in the adult. In the fixed helmet, reweighting each head's targets to the adult's depth distribution lowers the difference of pooled medians from the adult: templates, pooled {{ F.g3b_templates_pooled_diff_range }} dB, reweighted {{ F.g3b_templates_reweighted_diff_range }} dB; children, pooled {{ F.g3b_children_pooled_diff_range }} dB, reweighted {{ F.g3b_children_reweighted_diff_range }} dB. Within depth strata, where Δ is the difference of the two heads' area-weighted medians, it is positive in every stratum of the scaled adults and templates ({{ F.g3b_templates_scaled_dense_vs_combined_ib_depth_delta_range }} dB; [@sfig-depth-matched]). For the individual children it is negative in {{ F.g3b_children_depth_10_30mm_delta_negative_count }} of the {{ F.g3b_children_depth_10_30mm_cells_n }} child-strata of {{ F.g3b_depth_span_10_30_mm }} mm depth ({{ F.g3b_children_dense_vs_combined_ib_depth_10_30mm_delta_range }} dB; no interval lies above zero, the largest lower bound being {{ F.g3b_children_depth_10_30mm_ci_lo_max }} dB) and positive deeper ({{ F.g3b_children_dense_vs_combined_ib_depth_30_60mm_delta_range }} dB). In this decomposition, with depths measured from the scalp used (unverified near the scalp in the children, and shifted by {{ F.rev_qc_children_targets_depth_change_p50_mm_range }} mm in the children, {{ F.rev_qc_adult_targets_depth_change_p50_mm }} mm in the adult and {{ F.wr_qc_templates_targets_depth_change_p50_mm_range }} mm in the templates if measured from the MRI boundary; section D.3), the children's fixed-helmet gain is matched by their shallower cortex; this describes the model's surfaces, not a tested mechanism. In the helmet fitted at the adult's gap the children's strata are negative throughout the depths that hold most of their cortex: for child A, Δ at the adult's gap is {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_depth_10_15mm_delta }} dB at {{ F.wr_cgap_depth_10_15_label }} mm, {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_depth_20_25mm_delta }} dB at {{ F.wr_cgap_depth_20_25_label }} mm and {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_depth_30_40mm_delta }} dB at {{ F.wr_cgap_depth_30_40_label }} mm, and its intervals include zero only from the {{ F.wr_cgap_depth_40_50_label }} mm stratum down. The two decompositions, fitting the helmet and matching depth, are not additive, and neither apportions the routes exactly; the fitted helmet is the main control because it matches the adult's median gap, although it changes the gap distribution, the placement and the coverage with it.

### E.5 The noise in the smaller heads, and the share of cortex where the OPM is ahead {#s-helmets-noise}

The noise differs between the classes under the convention that the background variance per unit cortical area is the adult's. ## F. Regions by head and the noise floor across heads {#s-regions-heads}

### F.1 Regions by head {#s-regions-by-head}

In the fixed helmet the dense array is ahead in every region of every head ({{ F.regh_top_vs_combined_cells_range }} dB over the cells of [@sfig-regions-heads]a), and a smaller head exceeds the adult's value for the same region in {{ F.regh_top_vs_combined_smaller_cells_above_adult_count }} of {{ F.regh_top_vs_combined_smaller_cells_n }} cells. Of the six lobes, the temporal lobe gains most in the infant templates ({{ F.regh_infant2yr_top_vs_combined_temporal }}, {{ F.regh_infant18mo_top_vs_combined_temporal }} and {{ F.regh_infant12mo_top_vs_combined_temporal }} dB at {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }} and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_age_months }} months, against {{ F.regh_adult_top_vs_combined_temporal }} dB in the adult). The parahippocampal and mesial temporal rows, the adult's lowest temporal rows ({{ F.regh_adult_top_vs_combined_parahippocampal }} and {{ F.regh_adult_top_vs_combined_mesial_temporal }} dB), are higher in every template and scaled adult; the mesial temporal group reaches {{ F.regh_templates_scaled_top_vs_combined_mesial_temporal_range }} dB there but {{ F.regh_children_top_vs_combined_mesial_temporal_range }} dB in the children, child C ({{ F.regh_child_c_top_vs_combined_mesial_temporal }} dB) lying below the adult. The cingulate is the lowest row in every head ({{ F.regh_adult_top_vs_combined_cingulate }} dB in the adult, {{ F.regh_smaller_top_vs_combined_cingulate_range }} dB in the smaller heads).

**Peak-channel SNR by head.** Against the magnetometers alone the dense array's peak-channel SNR is ahead in every head (D {{ F.g3b_adult_dense_vs_mag_ib_peak_d }} dB in the adult at top contact, {{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_vs_mag_ib_peak_d_range }} dB in the smaller heads; sensor plus brain noise). Against Neuromag's best channel Neuromag leads in the adult ({{ F.g3b_adult_dense_vs_combined_ib_peak_d }} dB) and in {{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_vs_combined_ib_peak_d_negative_count }} of the {{ F.g4_n_smaller_heads }} smaller heads ({{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_vs_combined_ib_peak_d_range }} dB), the children among them ({{ F.g3b_child_a_dense_vs_combined_ib_peak_d }}, {{ F.g3b_child_b_dense_vs_combined_ib_peak_d }} and {{ F.g3b_child_c_dense_vs_combined_ib_peak_d }} dB in A, B and C).

Against the adult in its own scaled helmet, {{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_smaller_lobe_cells_at_or_below_adult_count }} of the {{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_smaller_lobe_cells_n }} lobe cells of the smaller heads are at or below the adult's; against the adult at top contact, {{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_smaller_lobe_cells_at_or_below_adult_top_count }} are ({{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_templates_scaled_lobe_cells_at_or_below_adult_top_count }} of {{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_templates_scaled_lobe_cells_n }} for the templates and scaled adults, {{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_children_lobe_cells_at_or_below_adult_top_count }} of {{ F.regh_cfx_vs_combined_children_lobe_cells_n }} for the children); these counts compare point estimates without a tolerance. The children's lobes range from {{ F.regh_children_cfx_vs_combined_lobes_range }} dB in the scaled helmet.

### F.2 The noise floor across heads {#s-noise-floor-heads}

D falls with the OPM's sensor noise in every head ([@sfig-noise-floor]). At {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz, about the empty-room floor read from the preprint's recording, the dense array's D is {{ F.g3b_adult_asd30_dense_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB in the adult and {{ F.g3b_smaller_asd30_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_range }} dB in the smaller heads; the site-matched array's is negative in every head except the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_years }}-year size control ({{ F.g3b_adult_asd30_matched_vs_combined_ib_d }} dB in the adult, {{ F.g3b_smaller_asd30_matched_vs_combined_ib_d_range }} dB in the smaller heads). The difference from the adult itself depends on the noise. At {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_min }} fT/√Hz the individual children are slightly below the adult ({{ F.g3b_children_asd7_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_range }} dB, difference of medians), at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz above it ({{ F.g3b_children_asd30_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_range }} dB). A plausible reading, not tested here, is that their OPM channels, dominated by brain noise, lose less to a noisier sensor than the adult's. A noisier OPM thus leaves the adult without an established advantage first, while the smaller heads in the fixed helmet keep part of theirs.

## G. The spike study: design, decisions and localization detail {#s-spikes}

### G.1 The exploratory design {#s-spikes-design}

**Time-domain model.** The detection study runs the noise model in the time domain: background moments with a 1/f spectrum, a room field with the cross-spectra of the empty-room recording and white sensor noise, each scaled so that its in-band covariance equals that of the adult model (the room field is not projected out), filtered to {{ F.g4_band_lo_hz }}–{{ F.g4_band_hi_hz }} Hz and decimated to {{ F.meth_td_fs_out_hz }} Hz, with one realization per {{ F.g4_segment_s }}-s segment shared by all arrays ([@stab-time-domain]). The spike is the spike-wave complex of Hunold et al. (2016), digitized, stretched in time by {{ F.g4_stretches }} ({{ F.g4_n_morphologies }} morphologies) and passed through the same filter.

**Events.** Spikes were placed at {{ F.g4_n_locations }} locations stratified by depth below the scalp ({{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }}, {{ F.g4_band_20_30mm }}, {{ F.g4_band_30_45mm }} and {{ F.g4_band_45_70mm }} mm; {{ F.g4_locations_per_band }} locations per band) and by orientation ({{ F.g4_n_orientation_bands }} bands from radial to tangential; {{ F.g4_locations_per_stratum }} locations per depth and orientation stratum). Each location received {{ F.g4_n_focal_events_per_location }} focal-dipole events ({{ F.g4_n_strengths }} strengths of {{ F.g4_strengths_nam }} nAm, each with every morphology) and patch events of {{ F.g4_patch_radius_mm }} mm radius carrying the same total moments with the unstretched morphology: {{ F.g4_n_focal_events }} focal and {{ F.g4_n_patch_events }} patch events, {{ F.g4_n_events_per_anatomy }} per anatomy, injected in random order one every {{ F.g4_event_spacing_s }} s, identically in every array. The adult's locations lie {{ F.g4_adult_locations_frontal }} in the frontal lobe, {{ F.g4_adult_locations_parietal }} parietal, {{ F.g4_adult_locations_temporal }} temporal, {{ F.g4_adult_locations_cingulate }} cingulate, {{ F.g4_adult_locations_insula }} insula and {{ F.g4_adult_locations_occipital }} occipital, at median depths of {{ F.g4_adult_median_depth_10_20mm }}, {{ F.g4_adult_median_depth_20_30mm }}, {{ F.g4_adult_median_depth_30_45mm }} and {{ F.g4_adult_median_depth_45_70mm }} mm in the four bands.

**Detectors.** Every array was read by two detectors, and Neuromag also by its magnetometers and its gradiometers alone ({{ F.g4_n_detectors }} sensor sets per anatomy, each read by both detectors). Both detectors whiten the data with a Ledoit–Wolf covariance from {{ F.g4_null_baseline_minutes }} min of null data and filter them with the spike templates. The oracle knows source and time: it tests the whitened, template-filtered projection on the true field pattern at the true peak (one-sided) against a threshold for a per-trial false-positive probability of {{ F.g4_oracle_alpha }}. The practical detector knows neither: at every sample it takes the largest normalized output over the {{ F.g4_n_morphologies }} templates and {{ F.g4_n_dictionary_range }} candidate field patterns on a {{ F.g4_dictionary_spacing_mm }}-mm cortical grid that excludes the true sources; its local maxima at least {{ F.g4_refractory_s }} s apart are events, and a spike counts as detected when an event above threshold lies within ±{{ F.g4_hit_tolerance_ms }} ms of its peak, the same events being counted as false on null data. The templates are the simulated morphologies and the candidates' field patterns come from the true forward model, which makes the practical detector idealized for every array; whether equally so was not tested in the exploratory run (the confirmatory run's mismatch variant tests one departure), and focal on-scalp patterns may be more sensitive to forward-model and coregistration error. Its thresholds for {{ F.g4_operating_point_per_minute }} false event per minute were set on {{ F.g4_null_calibration_minutes }} min of independent null data and frozen; {{ F.g4_null_heldout_minutes }} min of held-out null data gave {{ F.g4_heldout_rate_range }} false events per minute across sensor sets and anatomies ([@stab-heldout-rates]). In the adult the practical detector needs {{ F.g4_adult_practical_over_oracle_s50_10_20mm_range }} times the oracle's strength for half detection at {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm.

**S50 and the tests.** S50 is the strength at which the detection rate of a band's focal events ({{ F.g4_n_focal_events_per_band }} events: the morphologies pooled over the band's {{ F.g4_locations_per_band }} locations) reaches {{ F.g4_s50_level }}, interpolated linearly in log strength between the tested strengths; the patch events enter neither S50 nor the paired tests. The paired ratio S50~Neuromag~/S50~OPM~ is above 1 when the OPM needs less strength (section A). The adult sits at its measured pose and the smaller heads at top contact; the measured pose gives the adult a D {{ F.g3b_adult_centred_minus_top_d_abs }} dB higher than top contact, so the adult's spike results are not placed by the same rule as the smaller heads'.

**Endpoints named after the analyses.** The exploratory endpoints were (i) the dense array's detectability ratio against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels in the adult; (ii) Δ of the dense array against the same comparator at top contact in the fixed helmet and in the helmet scaled with the head about the laterally centred head, each against the adult placed by the same rule, reported descriptively; and (iii) for the spikes, the dense array against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels in the {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm band with the practical detector, tested with the two-sided exact sign-flip test on per-location differences in detection counts, Holm-adjusted (Holm, 1979) over the {{ F.g4_n_anatomies }} anatomies, with the paired S50 ratio and its location-bootstrap interval as the effect size. This band, array and detector were chosen from the {{ F.g4_family_per_opm_array }} comparisons per OPM array of each anatomy, a selection that the correction over anatomies does not control. The detection family has {{ F.g4_family_per_anatomy }} comparisons per anatomy (two OPM arrays against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels, its gradiometers and its magnetometers, with both detectors, in {{ F.g4_n_depth_bands }} depth bands) and the localization family {{ F.loc_family_per_anatomy }} (two OPM arrays; focal and patch sources at two strengths; the dipole error, the two dSPM errors, and joint detection and localization with the study's dSPM or with the dipole).

**What the location design allows.** The sign-flip test uses the locations whose difference in detection counts is not zero, and p is smallest when all favour one array: {{ F.meth_signflip_min_p_18 }} with a band's {{ F.meth_locs_per_band }} locations; p < {{ F.meth_alpha }} needs at least {{ F.meth_signflip_min_n }} such locations. With equal differences the test is the exact sign test: of {{ F.meth_locs_per_band }} locations, {{ F.meth_signtest_18_k }} must favour one array for p < {{ F.meth_alpha }} (p = {{ F.meth_signtest_18_k_p }}; with {{ F.meth_signtest_18_kminus1 }}, {{ F.meth_signtest_18_kminus1_p }}) and {{ F.meth_signtest_18_k_holm }} for the first Holm step over {{ F.meth_n_anatomies }} anatomies, p < {{ F.meth_holm_first_alpha }} (p = {{ F.meth_signtest_18_k_holm_p }}); of the confirmatory {{ F.meth_confirm_locs }} ({{ F.meth_confirm_locs_per_stratum }} per orientation stratum), {{ F.meth_signtest_36_k }} (p = {{ F.meth_signtest_36_k_p }}) and {{ F.meth_signtest_36_k_holm }} (p = {{ F.meth_signtest_36_k_holm_p }}). ### G.3 Localization {#s-spikes-localization}

**Design.** Localization was examined on {{ F.loc_n_locations }} locations with focal and patch sources of {{ F.loc_strengths_nam }} nAm, one event per source type and strength at each location ({{ F.loc_events_per_condition }} per condition, unstretched morphology) in independent null data. The inverse used a single-compartment BEM (the simulation used three layers), one of {{ F.loc_coreg_draws }} coregistration-error draws of {{ F.loc_coreg_shift_mm }} mm and {{ F.loc_coreg_angle_deg }}° per location, the same for every array (a median displacement of {{ F.loc_adult_coreg_displacement_mm }} mm of the adult's sources; {{ F.loc_coreg_displacement_mm_range }} mm over the anatomies), a Ledoit–Wolf noise covariance from {{ F.loc_baseline_minutes }} min of null data and a fixed-orientation source grid at {{ F.loc_grid_spacing_mm }}-mm spacing ({{ F.loc_inverse_grid_range }} sources) that excludes the true source vertices. Dipoles were fitted with MNE-Python at least {{ F.loc_ecd_min_dist_mm }} mm inside the inner skull, and dynamic statistical parametric mapping (dSPM; Dale et al., 2000), with depth weighting {{ F.loc_depth_weighting }} and a regularization set by an assumed SNR of {{ F.loc_snr }}, was computed in the study's implementation and in MNE-Python's, at the true peak sample. The error is the distance from the true source (a patch's centre) to the dipole or to the dSPM peak, read on the MRI through the perturbed transform; joint success is detection by the practical detector (thresholds from {{ F.loc_calibration_minutes }} min of null data) with an error of at most {{ F.loc_joint_threshold_mm }} mm. Paired error differences were tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and joint success with an exact McNemar test.

**The two dSPM implementations.** Both use fixed orientations on the same grid, the same noise covariance, a depth exponent of {{ F.meth_dspm_depth }}, λ² = {{ F.meth_dspm_lambda2 }}, a source covariance scaled to the whitened rank and dSPM normalization. They differ in the depth weighting: the study's uses the whitened gains of all of an array's channels, without a limit; MNE-Python's is limited at its default of {{ F.meth_mne_depth_limit }} and uses the unwhitened gains of the gradiometers, or of the magnetometers where there are none (the OPM arrays). Both are reported, because the paired dSPM differences depend on this choice.

**Results.** Dipole fits of detected {{ F.loc_strength_high_nam }}-nAm focal spikes have median errors of {{ F.loc_ecd_det_focal320_mm_range }} mm in every array and anatomy. Of {{ F.loc_family_n }} paired comparisons, {{ F.loc_family_n_sig }} have p < {{ F.wr_p_exploratory_threshold }}, most of them for dSPM errors ({{ F.loc_family_n_sig_dspm }} with the study's implementation, {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_dspm_mne }} with MNE-Python's), {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_ecd }} for dipole errors and {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_joint_dspm }} for joint detection and dSPM localization within {{ F.loc_joint_threshold_mm }} mm; {{ F.loc_family_expected_by_chance }} would be expected by chance. Of these, {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_favour_opm }} favour an OPM array, {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_favour_zero }} have a zero median difference and {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_favour_squid }} favours Neuromag; {{ F.loc_family_n_sig_80nam }} concern mostly undetected {{ F.loc_strength_low_nam }}-nAm sources, and the two dSPM implementations share their events. In all, {{ F.loc_survivors_bonferroni_20 }} comparisons survive a Bonferroni correction within anatomy and array, {{ F.loc_survivors_bonferroni_20_n_80nam }} of them at {{ F.loc_strength_low_nam }} nAm, where most sources go undetected, and {{ F.loc_survivors_bonferroni_all }} survive one over all {{ F.loc_family_n }}. Of the {{ F.wr_locfig_n_error_comparisons }} error comparisons, {{ F.wr_locfig_n_p_below_005 }} have p < {{ F.wr_p_exploratory_threshold }} ({{ F.wr_locfig_n_p_below_005_ecd }} for the dipole fit, {{ F.wr_locfig_n_p_below_005_dspm_mne }} for MNE-Python's dSPM, {{ F.wr_locfig_n_p_below_005_dspm }} for the study's) and {{ F.wr_locfig_n_p_below_bonferroni }} fall below {{ F.wr_locfig_bonferroni_threshold }}, the Bonferroni threshold for the {{ F.wr_locfig_family_per_anatomy_and_array }} comparisons of one anatomy and OPM array. ### G.4 Head motion {#s-spikes-motion}

Head motion and slippage of the OPM cap were bounded rather than simulated in time, on the adult and the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}- and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_age_months }}-month templates at top contact: the head was displaced in the fixed helmet by up to {{ F.mot_translation_largest_mm }} mm or rotated about the head origin, and the dense OPM cap rotated on the head by up to {{ F.mot_slip_largest_deg }}°, sensors that would enter the scalp being lifted; each case was evaluated with the matched filter of the reference geometry (uncompensated) and with the displaced geometry known ([S5](epilepsy.html)). A sustained {{ F.mot_translation_largest_mm }}-mm drop in the fixed helmet costs Neuromag {{ F.mot_squid_down10mm_mismatched_loss_db_range }} dB uncompensated and {{ F.mot_squid_down10mm_known_loss_db_range }} dB with the displaced geometry known, the latter comparable to an uncompensated {{ F.mot_slip_largest_deg }}° slip of the OPM cap ({{ F.mot_opm_slip3deg_mismatched_loss_db_range }} dB). A {{ F.mot_tc_duration_s }}-s time course of exact rigid motion checked the field-artefact model only ([S5](epilepsy.html)); motion in the spike simulations, and its interaction with detection thresholds, was not simulated.

### G.5 The confirmatory run {#s-spikes-confirmatory}

The confirmatory design, fixed before the run, is stated in the main text (Section 2.5). Its declarations in full:

- seeds: a root seed ({{ F.meth_seed_confirm_root }}), from which one stream per anatomy and purpose is spawned;
- locations: {{ F.meth_confirm_locs }} newly drawn per anatomy in the {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm band ({{ F.meth_confirm_locs_per_stratum }} per orientation stratum), vertex-disjoint from the exploratory ones where a stratum allows;
- test: the two-sided sign-flip test, exact up to {{ F.meth_signflip_exact_max }} non-zero location differences and otherwise estimated from {{ F.meth_signflip_mc_patterns }} random sign patterns;
- effect size: the paired S50 ratio with a location-bootstrap interval of {{ F.meth_boot_confirm }} resamples;
- null data: separate segments for the whitener, for fitting the thresholds, for the held-out check and for a {{ F.meth_null_evaluation_min }}-min evaluation of the false-event rates of the frozen and the rate-matched thresholds;

## H. Analysis history {#s-history}

This note collects, in one place, what was decided before and after results were known, so that the reader can tell a pre-specified result from an exploratory one.

**Named before any run.** The analysis configuration files of the adult and pediatric studies named two noise conditions before any simulation: sensor plus brain noise, the primary one, and the room field projected out. The whole-cortex results are reported in both conditions in the main text and here, the regional results with sensor plus brain noise; the Abstract and Conclusions lead with sensor plus brain noise.

**Named after the analyses.** No endpoint, test or margin of the original study was declared before its analyses. The exploratory spike endpoint (the dense array against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels at {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm with the practical detector) was chosen from {{ F.g4_family_per_opm_array }} comparisons per OPM array (section G.1). The regions were chosen from the epilepsy literature (section D.2), and rankings among them are exploratory. The primary placement of the fixed helmet, top contact, was chosen after the adult's whole-cortex results were known, and the laterally centred placement and the helmet scaled about the laterally centred head were added after the first pediatric results, to remove the templates' lateral offset (section E.2); the 12 source-blind placements (section E.1) bound the first choice. The main text states the post hoc caveat once (Section 2.6) and reports every exploratory result as such.

**Model versions.** The model went through four versions before the results were frozen. The first three took the OPM sensitive axes from the adult's stored outer-skin normals, which are nearly radial, and placed the adult's head surface outside its MRI scalp (by a median of {{ F.g2_conform_median_mm }} mm, the move of the final version's conform step); the second checked the OPM cell's clearance from the head at its integration points only, and the third at the whole cell, which moved most of the adult's sites outward. The final version puts every head's surface on its MRI scalp, so that the clearance rule moves only the sites the anatomy demands and the OPM standoff is the same ({{ F.g3b_all_dense_standoff_2dp_range }} mm) above every head's own MRI scalp (the templates' scalp convention differs from the adult's; section D.3), and takes the sensitive axes from that surface's own triangles (section C.1). This last change, equal OPM standoff on every head, was made after the pediatric results were known. It lowered Δ by {{ F.g3b_v3_v4_delta_drop_range }} dB in the scaled adults and templates (the school-aged children were added afterwards and exist only in the final version), raised the adult's projected dense ratio (the earlier construction gave {{ F.g2_hse_v3_dense_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }}; the final sites with the earlier, nearly radial axes give {{ F.g2_hse_v4sites_v3axes_dense_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }}; the final model gives {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }}), made the laterally centred scaled-helmet Δ of the scaled adults and templates negative, and strengthened the adult's exploratory spike result at {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm (earlier {{ F.g4_earlier_v3_dense_vs_combined_10_20mm }} locations, p = {{ F.g4_earlier_v3_dense_vs_combined_10_20mm_p }}, S50 ratio {{ F.g4_earlier_v3_dense_vs_combined_10_20mm_ratio }}, against {{ F.g4_adult_dense_vs_combined_practical_10_20mm_locs_opm }}/{{ F.g4_adult_dense_vs_combined_practical_10_20mm_locs_squid }} and {{ F.g4_adult_dense_vs_combined_practical_10_20mm_ratio }} in the final version). Every array change redraws the noise, because an array's channel count changes the random stream. The dense array's advantage at {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm held in every version; which deeper cells reach p < {{ F.wr_p_exploratory_threshold }} did not: the site-matched array's deficit at {{ F.g4_band_45_70mm }} mm in two earlier versions ({{ F.g4_earlier_v2_matched_vs_combined_45_70mm }} locations in the second, {{ F.g4_earlier_v3_matched_vs_combined_45_70mm }} with p = {{ F.g4_earlier_v3_matched_vs_combined_45_70mm_p }} in the third) does not reach p < {{ F.wr_p_exploratory_threshold }} in the final one ({{ F.g4_adult_matched_vs_combined_practical_45_70mm_locs_opm }}/{{ F.g4_adult_matched_vs_combined_practical_45_70mm_locs_squid }} locations, p = {{ F.g4_adult_matched_vs_combined_practical_45_70mm_p }}). Results that depend on these choices are post hoc, the earlier result files are not part of the stored results, and persistence across versions is not replication.

**Added in the revision.** In the revision, analyses were added without changing the model: the check of the noise model against the measured Neuromag covariance and the sensitivity analyses (section C), the MRI quality check of the school-aged children (section D.3) and the helmet fitted at the adult's gap (section E.3). Each reproduces the stored results it builds on to within rounding, as its result file records, and each was run at one code commit named in that file. The confirmatory spike run was designed before it was run (main text, Section 2.5) and is the only pre-specified test of the study.
