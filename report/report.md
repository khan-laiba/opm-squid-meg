# Brain region, head size and helmet fit in on-scalp versus SQUID MEG: a simulation study from adults to children, with simulated interictal spikes

**Laiba Khan**

**Highlights**

- In the tested conditions, OPM noise or array choice can reverse which system leads
- In the model a dense OPM array leads Neuromag; a site-matched one shows no clear lead
- That lead is within the model's error for Neuromag; OPM noise remains unmeasured
- Fitting the helmet to the adult's median gap shrinks the smaller heads' extra lead
- With an idealized detector, dense OPMs detected weaker shallow simulated spikes

**Abstract**

On-scalp optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs) record larger fields than cryogenic SQUID magnetoencephalography (MEG), but in a spherical model they lead only for sources shallower than a depth set by an assumed noise ratio. We replaced that ratio with explicit sensor, brain and room noise, compared the Neuromag system with a dense and a site-matched OPM array by known-topography detectability in an adult and {{ F.wr_n_smaller_heads_words }} smaller heads, and simulated interictal spikes. In the adult, the OPM noise floor or the array could reverse which system led: with sensor plus brain noise a dense {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }}-fT/√Hz array had {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }} times Neuromag's detectability (no established lead at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz), and the site-matched array showed no established advantage. The dense lead survived the omissions tested but is no larger than the model's error for Neuromag (assumptions about each system's real noise give {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s3_ratio }} to {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s2_ratio }}): a conditional prediction until OPM noise is measured. Under the modelled noise (exploratory), the dense lead was smallest in cingulate cortex and the site-matched array weakest on ventral and polar surfaces. In the fixed adult helmet the scaled adults' and infant templates' lead exceeded the adult's by {{ F.g3b_templates_scaled_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB; fitting the helmet to the adult's median gap removed most of this extra lead (an interaction of {{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_interaction_range }} dB). The school-aged children, whose near-scalp anatomy could not be verified, are provisional examples. In a confirmatory run (modelled noise, idealized detector, fixed adult helmet), the dense array detected simulated spikes {{ F.cf_band }} mm deep at {{ F.cf_all_dense_vs_combined_practical_reduction_pct_range }} lower strength than Neuromag in all {{ F.wr_n_anatomies_words }} anatomies (not independent replications); no localization difference was established (exploratory). Clinical reports of higher OPM SNR are compatible with these conditions but not explained by them.

## 1. Introduction {#intro}

Optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs) measure the brain's magnetic field at room temperature and can be worn on the scalp, whereas the superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs) of conventional magnetoencephalography (MEG) sit in a rigid cryogenic helmet (Boto et al., 2017; Iivanainen et al., 2017). The helmet is built for adults: a child's head sits in it with a wide and uneven gap, whereas an on-scalp array fits any head (Hill et al., 2019; Feys & De Tiège, 2024). Clinical OPM studies of epilepsy report larger interictal discharges than cryogenic MEG (Feys et al., 2022). Their signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) are higher in some comparisons (Feys et al., 2022, 2025; Ren et al., 2025, abstract only) and equal or slightly lower in others (Hillebrand et al., 2023; Schwartz et al., 2025), and their weakest results concern temporal and mesial temporal sources (Feys et al., 2024, 2025; Shen et al., 2026).

A larger field does not by itself give a higher SNR. Jas et al. (2026) showed this in a spherical head with a point magnetometer on the scalp (OPM) and {{ F.lit_xi_squid_mm }} mm above it (SQUID): if the OPM's noise is η times the SQUID's, its single-channel SNR is higher only for sources shallower than an equal-SNR depth, about {{ F.lit_deq_eta3_printed_mm }} mm at η = {{ F.lit_eta_reference }} in an adult. With a SQUID shell that follows the head at a constant standoff, that depth covers a larger fraction of a small brain, a predicted benefit for children.

A realistic comparison needs explicit noise seen through each array's own lead fields, finite arrays on a real scalp and a real helmet around a smaller head. With sensor noise only, a helium-4 OPM array's advantage over SQUID magnetometers grew as the head shrank, in a standard adult placement and in a helmet fitted at a constant gap (Zahran et al., 2022). With brain noise on one adult, simulated spikes were visible on the scalp from smaller cortical patches than with conventional MEG (Westin et al., 2023). With brain noise but no sensor noise, OPMs at the SQUID sites of epilepsy patients raised a channel-averaged SNR in no region, though the OPM–SQUID difference correlated with the sensor-distance ratio, which followed head circumference (Matsubara et al., 2026). None compared head size and helmet fit with brain noise, fitted a helmet at the adult's own median gap (Zahran et al.'s constant gap is their adult's closest distance), or checked its noise model against a measured covariance; this study does all three, although its fitted helmet changes gap, placement and coverage together.

We therefore ask four questions. (Q1) What decides the comparison between OPM arrays and the Neuromag system on a real adult head with explicit noise, and how far can the noise model be trusted? (Q2) Which brain regions favour the OPM, and where does a site-matched array fall behind? (Q3) Is the larger relative advantage expected in smaller heads a matter of head size or of the fixed adult helmet? (Q4) Does a detectability difference change which simulated interictal spikes are detected, and how well they are localized?

## 2. Methods {#methods}

### 2.1 Study design and estimands {#meth-design}

The primary metric is known-topography detectability, d = √(sᵀC⁻¹s): the output SNR of the matched filter for a source of known field pattern s in noise of covariance C, with the true (oracle) covariance. Arrays are compared target by target by the paired ratio d~OPM~/d~Neuromag~, or by D = 20 log~10~ of it in dB, positive when the OPM is ahead. For the adult we report the unweighted median over all {{ F.g2_n_targets }} targets, the {{ F.g2_medialwall_n }} on the medial wall included, as a ratio (the medial wall is not cortex; without it the dense array's ratio is {{ F.wr_g2_dense_ib_ratio_no_medial_wall }}). For the smaller heads, whose cortices differ in size, we report D as an area-weighted median without the medial wall, and Δ = D~head~ − D~adult~ with the adult placed by the same helmet rule. ### 2.2 Anatomies {#meth-anatomies}

The adult is the MNE sample subject (Gramfort et al., 2013) with FreeSurfer surfaces and atlas labels (Fischl, 2012; Desikan et al., 2006). Targets are the usable vertices of an oct-6 source space on the white surface at least {{ F.g2_source_skull_clearance_mm }} mm inside the inner skull, each a {{ F.g2_focal_nam }}-nAm dipole normal to the cortex; depth is the distance to the nearest point of the MRI scalp; the three-layer boundary-element model (BEM; Hämäläinen & Sarvas, 1989; conductivities {{ F.g2_conductivity }} S/m) has its outer surface on the MRI scalp.

The {{ F.wr_n_smaller_heads_words }} smaller heads fall into three classes ([@tab-anatomies]): {{ F.wr_n_scaled_words }} **scaled adults** (copies of the adult scaled to school-age size and to the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}-month template's head circumference), {{ F.wr_n_templates_words }} **infant templates** at {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }} and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_age_months }} months (averages of many MRIs; O'Reilly et al., 2021; Richards et al., 2016) and {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} typically developing **school-aged children**, A, B and C ({{ F.g3b_children_age_range }} years; OpenNeuro ds005234, Fadeev et al., 2024, 2025). The children's inner skull was moved inward toward {{ F.g3b_cfg_child_skull_depth }} mm below the scalp wherever the dataset's BEM surfaces lay shallower, but kept at least {{ F.g3b_cfg_child_skull_min_cortex }} mm from every white-surface vertex, so that over the upper head it lies a median {{ F.g3b_children_modelled_inner_skull_depth_p50_range }} mm below the scalp (adult {{ F.g3b_adult_inner_skull_depth_p50 }} mm), and their fiducials were transferred from the adult by a similarity transform fitted between the adult's and the child's white surfaces (trimmed iterative closest points) and moved to the nearest scalp vertex, a method that reproduces the infant templates' own fiducials to within {{ F.g3b_templates_fid_transfer_range }} mm and their head frames to {{ F.g3b_templates_fid_transfer_rotation_range }}° ([S1, section D](supplement.html#s-anatomies)). Because their white surfaces come within {{ F.rev_qc_children_white_scalp_min_mm_range }} mm of the scalp, the surfaces were checked against the MRIs ([@sfig-children-qc]). The FreeSurfer scalp lies about as far inside the T1 head boundary in the children (median {{ F.wr_qc_children_offset_inside_otsu_mm_range }} mm) as in the adult ({{ F.wr_qc_adult_offset_inside_otsu_mm }} mm), and the white surfaces are registered to the T1, so the shallow cortex is not a scalp-placement or registration error. The check cannot verify the white surface within a few millimetres of the scalp, nor the transferred fiducials; the count of shallow targets depends on the scalp definition (measured to the MRI head boundary, child B has {{ F.wr_qc_child_b_targets_lt10mm_mri_n }} instead of {{ F.g3b_child_b_shallow_targets_lt10mm }} targets less than 10 mm deep); and the source rule leaves out {{ F.wr_excl_children_pct_range }} of the children's cortex (adult {{ F.wr_excl_adult_pct }}), including {{ F.wr_excl_children_lt8mm_pct_range }} of their cortex within 8 mm of the scalp. The children's results are therefore provisional geometric examples, not evidence for a mechanism. The infant templates are averages: the 24-month template's scalp lies {{ F.rev_qc_infant2yr_outward_mm }} mm outside its own MRI head boundary, where the adult's lies inside, so that relative to that boundary both systems' sensors sit farther from the template's brain than from the adult's, which lowers the template's D and Δ; the 18- and 12-month templates were not checked. All heads use the adult's conductivities; a single-compartment BEM changes each smaller head's median D minus the adult's by {{ F.meth_bem1_change_db_range }} dB.

### 2.3 Arrays and helmets {#meth-arrays}

**Neuromag ({{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels)** combines the sample recording's VectorView geometry, MNE-Python's T3 coil definitions (types {{ F.meth_coil_grad_type }} and {{ F.meth_coil_mag_type }}) and the TRIUX datasheet's typical white noise, {{ F.meth_squid_mag_asd }} fT/√Hz and {{ F.meth_squid_grad_asd }} fT/(cm √Hz), with its {{ F.meth_dewar_mm }}-mm Dewar spacing between coils and helmet (Elekta Neuromag, n.d.); no signal-space separation is applied.

**OPM arrays** ([@fig-arrays]a to c). Each OPM is a single-axis sensor with its sensing centre nominally {{ F.g2_dense_standoff_mm }} mm above the MRI scalp (Osborne et al., 2018; Alem et al., 2023; a few sites moved outward for clearance) and a {{ F.g2_opm_cell_mm }}-mm cubic averaging volume, a modelling choice that changes the peak field by at most {{ F.wr_opm_cell_peak_effect_pct }} ([S7](register.html)). Its axis is the head-surface normal averaged within {{ F.meth_opm_axis_radius_mm }} mm of the site (nearly radial axes give a projected dense ratio of {{ F.meth_hse_radial_dense_proj_ratio_3dp }} instead of {{ F.meth_hse_final_dense_proj_ratio_3dp }}; [S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)). Above the preauricular plane, the **dense OPM array** fills the scalp at a minimum spacing of {{ F.g2_dense_packing_mm }} mm ({{ F.g2_dense_sites }} sites on the adult, more sites than any clinical OPM array), and the **site-matched OPM array** puts one OPM at each Neuromag site inside the region, projected onto the scalp ({{ F.g2_matched_sites }} sites). On every head the dense array is rebuilt by these rules ({{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_sites_range }} sites on the smaller heads) and the site-matched array takes that head's Neuromag sites at top contact ({{ F.g3b_smaller_matched_sites_range }} sites; [@tab-anatomies]). Clinical systems reach about 100 to 128 channels with dual- or triaxial sensors at fewer sites (Feys et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2026); such arrays are not modelled, and a triaxial array at the site-matched sites ({{ F.g2_triax_channels }} channels) is a control for the gain from extra axes, not a model of them. The OPM white noise is {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz in the primary analysis, flat over {{ F.g2_band_range_hz }} Hz, and is swept over {{ F.wr_opm_asd_sweep_list }} fT/√Hz (the clinical noise figures state no band; [S7](register.html)).

The **fixed adult helmet** ([@fig-arrays]d) keeps the adult's sensors and noise for every head. The primary placement is *top contact*: from the pose that puts the head's frame where the adult's was recorded, the head is raised until the nearest magnetometer coil is {{ F.g3b_cfg_clearance }} mm from the scalp, a best-fit assumption that favours Neuromag (Gaetz et al., 2008; Tesan et al., 2010; S1, section E). A family of {{ F.g3b_n_family_placements }} source-blind placements, chosen from the geometry alone (contacts, shifts and rotations; [S1, section E](supplement.html#s-helmets)), gives the placement band.

**Helmet fitted at the adult's gap.** Each head is *laterally centred* (moved sideways to the helmet's midline), and the helmet scaled about the head origin until the median magnetometer-to-scalp gap equals the adult's in the same pose, {{ F.rev_cgap_adult_gap_mm }} mm. No coil may come within the {{ F.g3b_cfg_dewar_spacing }}-mm Dewar spacing of the scalp; in child C this limit binds first, at {{ F.rev_cgap_child_c_fitted_gap_mm }} mm. The **helmet scaled with the head**, which scales the gap too, is a secondary control ([S1, section E](supplement.html#s-helmets)), and an **equal-site-count control** subsamples the adult's dense array to each smaller head's site count. None models a pediatric SQUID device.

### 2.4 Noise model, validation and sensitivity analyses {#meth-noise}

Every array sees identical noise sources in one band of {{ F.g2_band_range_hz }} Hz. Each array's oracle covariance is computed in closed form as C = Σ~s~ + s² L~bg~ A L~bg~^T^ + E Σ~e~ E^T^, where Σ~s~ is the diagonal white sensor noise, L~bg~ holds the lead fields of {{ F.g2_n_background }} independent cortex-normal background dipoles on a {{ F.g2_bg_grid_mm }}-mm grid, A is the diagonal matrix of their Voronoi-cell areas, and E and Σ~e~ are the array's responses to {{ F.g2_room_terms }} external-field terms and their coefficient covariance. Each background dipole's moment variance is s² times its area (s² = {{ F.meth_bg_var_per_mm2_nam2 }} nAm² per mm²); like the targets, the background omits the cortex within {{ F.g2_source_skull_clearance_mm }} mm of the inner skull. Two quantities are fitted to the sample dataset: Σ~e~ (to the empty-room recording) and the scalar s², so that the median good gradiometer's modelled brain noise equals the measured one (an RMS of {{ F.meth_bg_calibration_grad_ft_cm }} fT/cm). Everything else is a prediction, except the declared sensor noise of both systems. The conditions are sensor noise only; sensor plus brain noise (primary); sensor, brain and room noise; and the last with the room field's subspace removed from every array by one noise-weighted projection ('projected').

**Validation.** The model's Neuromag covariance was compared with the measured one of the same recording, and Neuromag's detectability recomputed with it ([S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)); as the OPM's covariance cannot be measured here, the implied OPM/Neuromag ratio was computed under five stated assumptions (scenarios A to E; Section 3.2).

**Sensitivity analyses**, each changing one element of the adult model and recalibrating s²: cortex {{ F.rev_ns_joint_floor_mm }} to {{ F.rev_ns_near_rule_mm }} mm from the inner skull in the background, as targets, or both; coloured OPM noise (1/f plus white, corners at {{ F.rev_ns_col_corners_hz }} Hz) under a matched filter that also whitens across frequency; idealized cardiac and ocular far-field sources; and all together.

### 2.5 Simulated interictal spikes {#meth-spikes}

The spike study runs the noise model in the time domain, in segments shared by all arrays, with the spike-wave complex of Hunold et al. (2016) stretched to {{ F.wr_n_morphologies_words }} morphologies as the spike. Two matched-filter detectors, whitened with a Ledoit–Wolf covariance (Ledoit & Wolf, 2004), read the data: the oracle knows source and time; the practical detector knows neither, scans candidate patterns on a {{ F.g4_dictionary_spacing_mm }}-mm grid without the true sources, and has thresholds for a nominal {{ F.g4_operating_point_per_minute }} false event per minute frozen on independent null data. Both are idealized: they use the true forward model and the simulated waveform family.

**Exploratory design.** Spikes were placed at {{ F.g4_n_locations }} locations stratified by depth ({{ F.wr_n_depth_bands_words }} bands from {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} to {{ F.g4_band_45_70mm }} mm, {{ F.g4_locations_per_band }} locations each) and orientation, each with {{ F.g4_n_focal_events_per_location }} focal events of {{ F.g4_strength_range }} nAm and patch events, in one noise realization per event, identical in every array. The adult sits at its measured head position here (D {{ F.g3b_adult_centred_minus_top_d_abs }} dB above top contact; [@tab-estimands] and Section 3.4), the smaller heads at top contact; every comparison is within one anatomy.

**Confirmatory design, fixed before the run.** The design was declared in `configs/g4_confirmatory.toml`, committed to the repository on 4 October 2026 (commit 10e37b9) before the run, which ran on 5 October at commit {{ F.cf_commit }} (the same code and configuration); the dates are those of the repository's history (both commits postdate its rewrite on 4 October 2026, which corrected the author identity of earlier commits; docs/commit_map.tsv), and the run had no external registration. The endpoint is the dense OPM array against Neuromag ({{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels) with the practical detector at a nominal {{ F.g4_operating_point_per_minute }} false event per minute (a threshold set on {{ F.cf_null_calibration_minutes }} min of calibration null rests on about {{ F.wr_confirm_calib_expected_events }} false events, a Poisson uncertainty of about {{ F.wr_confirm_calib_poisson_pct }}), for focal spikes {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm below the scalp in all {{ F.wr_n_anatomies_words }} anatomies: the two-sided sign-flip test on per-location differences in detection counts, Holm-adjusted over the anatomies (Holm, 1979) at α = {{ F.meth_alpha }}, with the paired S50 ratio r as the effect size (the OPM's 50% detection strength is lower by 1 − 1/r). The run draws {{ F.meth_confirm_locs }} new locations per anatomy and {{ F.wr_confirm_replicates_words }} noise replicates per event, the first carrying the endpoint, and uses separate null data for threshold fitting and rate evaluation. The {{ F.meth_confirm_locs }} locations double the exploratory {{ F.wr_confirm_pilot_locs_per_band }}: {{ F.wr_confirm_pilot_runs_words }} pilot runs on test seeds in child A ({{ F.wr_confirm_pilot_locs }} locations favouring the OPM : Neuromag, p = {{ F.wr_confirm_pilot_p }}; recorded in the declaration, their outputs not kept; [S1, section G](supplement.html#s-spikes)) prompted the doubling but do not estimate power. Secondary analyses: a declared mismatched detector (off-template morphologies, a single-compartment BEM, a shared coregistration error of {{ F.wr_confirm_coreg_shift_mm }} mm and {{ F.wr_confirm_coreg_angle_deg }}°), the site-matched array, the oracle and rate-matched thresholds (re-fitted on held-out null data).

**Localization**, in the exploratory run, used equivalent-current-dipole fits and dynamic statistical parametric mapping (dSPM; Dale et al., 2000) at the true peak sample on {{ F.loc_n_locations }} locations, through a single-compartment BEM with a coregistration error of {{ F.loc_coreg_shift_mm }} mm and {{ F.loc_coreg_angle_deg }}° ([S1, section G](supplement.html#s-spikes)).

### 2.6 Statistics {#meth-stats}

Neighbouring targets are correlated, so intervals are {{ F.g2_ci_level_pct }} percentile bootstrap intervals over the adult's {{ F.g2_n_parcels }} atlas labels or over each smaller head's parcels (resample counts: [@stab-resamples]); they describe spatial variation within one anatomy. In the spike study the location is the unit of resampling and testing. The exploratory spike endpoint (chosen from {{ F.g4_family_per_opm_array }} comparisons per OPM array), the primary helmet placement and the regions were named after the whole-cortex results were known, and the model was revised while results were known ([S1, section H](supplement.html#s-history)); only the confirmatory run carries a pre-specified test.

Simulations used MNE-Python {{ F.g3b_mne_version }} (Gramfort et al., 2013) and NumPy {{ F.g3b_numpy_version }}, with the seeds listed in [S1, section I](supplement.html#s-tables).

## 3. Results {#results}

### 3.1 What decides the adult comparison {#res-conditions}

*With explicit noise, which array detects a cortical source better, and what decides it?* With sensor noise alone Neuromag leads both OPM arrays, the median paired ratio over the {{ F.g2_n_targets }} targets being {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_int_ratio }} (dense) and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_int_ratio }} (site-matched; [@fig-conditions]). With the room field included, brain noise is {{ F.g2_noise_share_dense_brain_pct }} of a dense-array OPM channel's in-band variance and {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_brain_pct }} of a Neuromag magnetometer's (room field {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_room_pct }}). With sensor plus brain noise the dense array's ratio is {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}, D = {{ F.wr_adult_dense_ib_db }} dB, and the OPM is ahead at all but {{ F.rev_g2_notahead_n }} targets. The site-matched array's ratio is {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}, D = {{ F.wr_adult_matched_ib_db }} dB: no established advantage.

The **comparator**: against the {{ F.g2_squid_grad_channels }} gradiometers alone the ratios are {{ F.g2_dense_vs_grad_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_grad_ib_ratio }} (dense, site-matched), against the {{ F.g2_squid_sites }} magnetometers {{ F.g2_dense_vs_mag_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_mag_ib_ratio }}. The **metric**: by peak-channel SNR, the closest analogue of clinical sensor-level SNRs (whose baselines also hold physiological noise; [S1, section A](supplement.html#s-estimands)), the dense array is ahead of the magnetometers ({{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_mag_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_mag_ib_ci }}) but behind the best of all {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels ({{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}), and by mean-power SNR its lead is small ({{ F.g2_meanpow_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). The **OPM noise floor**: from {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_min }} to {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz the dense ratio falls from {{ F.g2_sens_asd7ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} to {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}, no established advantage, and the site-matched ratio from {{ F.g2_sens_asd7ft_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} to {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }}; the break-even white noise is {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_ib_asd }} {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_ib_asd_ci }} fT/√Hz (dense) and {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_matched_vs_combined_ib_asd }} {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_matched_vs_combined_ib_asd_ci }} fT/√Hz (site-matched; [@fig-noise-checks]d).

Other choices move the dense ratio less ([@fig-conditions]). Projecting out the room field gives {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_proj_ci_3dp }}, and the site-matched array then trails Neuromag ({{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_proj_ci_3dp }}). A {{ F.g2_opm_gap_small_mm }}- or {{ F.g2_opm_gap_large_mm }}-mm scalp gap lowers the dense ratio to {{ F.g2_sens_gap3mm_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} or {{ F.g2_sens_gap6mm_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}, and {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz with a {{ F.g2_opm_gap_small_mm }}-mm gap reverses it ({{ F.g2_joint_gap3mm_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_joint_gap3mm_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}). A triaxial array at the site-matched sites recovers much of the lead ({{ F.g2_triax_equal_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). With a covariance estimated from {{ F.g2_plugin60s_samples }} samples, the realistic operating point, the ratios are {{ F.g2_plugin60s_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_plugin60s_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }} and {{ F.g2_plugin60s_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_plugin60s_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci }}. Across these conditions, then, the OPM noise floor or the array can reverse which system leads, the comparator and the metric decide which reported number shows it, and depth changes the size of the dense lead and the sign of the site-matched array's (Section 3.3).

### 3.2 How far the noise model can be trusted {#res-noise}

*What does the model get right about Neuromag's noise, and what does that imply for the OPM?* The model predicts {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_amp_ratio }} of the magnetometers' measured brain-noise amplitude, which is not fitted (Section 2.4; [@sfig-noise-budget]), or {{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_amp_without_ratio }} with the heart's field removed. The gradiometer level at randomly held-out sites ({{ F.rev_cov_heldout_random_grad_amp_ratio }}; {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_random_grad_amp_ci }} over {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_n_splits }} splits) and the overall pattern of channel correlations agree, the former nearly by construction. The modelled brain noise is, however, too flat from bottom to top ({{ F.rev_cov_heldout_inf_to_sup_grad_amp_ratio }} and {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_sup_to_inf_grad_amp_ratio }} when fitted on the inferior or superior half), its magnetometer correlations and per-channel variances are poorly predicted, and the magnetometers' excess over the model sits in a few far-field components ([S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)). For detectability this excess favours Neuromag: with its measured covariance Neuromag's detectability is {{ F.rev_cov_det_combined_corrected_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_det_combined_corrected_ci }} times its modelled value ([@fig-noise-checks]a), a factor as large as the dense array's whole lead.

What this implies for the OPM depends on its real noise, which no measurement here constrains ([@fig-noise-checks]b): if the model's error is common to both systems (scenario A), the ratios stay at {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s1_ratio }} and {{ F.rev_cov_opm_matched_vs_combined_s1_ratio }}, and nearly so under scenarios B and E ([S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)); if Neuromag is taken as measured and the OPM as modelled (scenario D), the dense ratio is {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ci }}, no established advantage, and the site-matched {{ F.rev_cov_opm_matched_vs_combined_s4_ratio }}; if the OPM bears the whole magnetometer variance excess as cortical noise (scenario C, the most pessimistic), {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s3_ratio }} and {{ F.rev_cov_opm_matched_vs_combined_s3_ratio }}.

The omissions tested move the lead little at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz ([@fig-noise-checks]c; [@stab-breakeven]): cortex {{ F.rev_ns_joint_floor_mm }} to {{ F.rev_ns_near_rule_mm }} mm from the inner skull gives {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_bg_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} (as background) to {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_targets_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} (as targets); a 1/f component cornering at {{ F.rev_ns_joint_corner_hz }} Hz gives {{ F.rev_ns_col_bv_asd15ft_c10hz_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} under the primary detector and {{ F.rev_ns_col_fr_asd15ft_c10hz_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} under one that also whitens across frequency. Cardiac and ocular sources change the dense ratio by {{ F.rev_ns_ff_dense_vs_combined_ib_change_pct_range }}. The joint run of near-skull background with the {{ F.rev_ns_joint_corner_hz }}-Hz corner, frequency-whitened ([@fig-noise-checks]d, dashed), gives {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd15ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd15ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }}, {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd20ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd20ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_20 }} and {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz: under it the lead is established up to {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} but not {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_20 }} fT/√Hz, against up to {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_20 }} ({{ F.g2_sens_asd20ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_sens_asd20ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}) but not {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz under the primary model (Section 3.1). A measured OPM covariance is the missing test.

### 3.3 Depth and regions {#res-regions}

*Where on the cortex does the dense array lead, and where does the site-matched array fall behind?* The lead is largest near the scalp ([@fig-adult-depth]a): the dense ratio is {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_10_15_ratio }} {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_10_15_ci }} in the {{ F.g2_depth_bin_10_15_mm }} mm bin, {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_25_30_ratio }} {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_25_30_ci }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_25_30_mm }} mm and {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_combined_ib_35_60_range }} from {{ F.g2_depth_bin_35_40_mm }} to {{ F.g2_depth_bin_55_60_mm }} mm, every interval above 1. The site-matched array has an equal-SNR depth even with sensor plus brain noise: its intervals lie above 1 down to the {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_above1_through_bin }} mm bin and below 1 from {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_below1_from_bin }} mm. With the room field projected out, the dense array's bin median falls below 1 from {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_proj_median_below1_from_bin }} mm and the site-matched array's from {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_proj_median_below1_from_bin }} mm. For the dense array the fall is geometric: it is present with sensor noise alone ([@fig-adult-depth]a, dotted) and in the peak field itself ([@fig-adult-depth]b), brain noise multiplying the ratio by a nearly constant factor in every bin ({{ F.g2_brainmult_dense_vs_combined_range }}). On the cortex ([@fig-adult-depth]c) the dense array is ahead almost everywhere, most on the lateral convexity.

By region ([@fig-regions-adult]) the dense lead is largest in the frontal lobe ({{ F.g2_lobe_frontal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_lobe_frontal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}) and smallest in the cingulate ({{ F.g2_lobe_cingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_lobe_cingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}) and the insula ({{ F.g2_lobe_insula_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). Of the regions chosen from the epilepsy literature, the precentral gyrus favours it most ({{ F.reg_precentral_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}) and the parahippocampal cortex least ({{ F.reg_parahippocampal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), though {{ F.reg_dk_dense_vs_combined_ib_below_parahippocampal_count }} atlas parcels, mostly cingulate and medial, have smaller leads. The site-matched array trails most on ventral and polar surfaces, e.g. the frontal pole ({{ F.reg_dk_frontalpole_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), medial and lateral orbitofrontal cortex ({{ F.reg_dk_medialorbitofrontal_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.reg_dk_lateralorbitofrontal_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}) and the temporal pole ({{ F.reg_dk_temporalpole_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), and, of the literature's regions, in mesial temporal cortex (parahippocampal and entorhinal; {{ F.reg_mesial_temporal_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). These rankings are exploratory point estimates of the model, conditional on the OPM's unmeasured noise: under scenario D (Section 3.2; Neuromag as measured, the OPM as modelled) the dense ratio becomes {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_cingulate_ratio }} in the cingulate, {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_parietal_ratio }} in the parietal, {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_frontal_ratio }} in the frontal and {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_temporal_ratio }} in the temporal lobe (point estimates), and falls below 1 from the {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_depth_first_below1_label }} mm bin (room field included).

### 3.5 Helmet fit versus head size {#res-fit}

*Is the smaller heads' larger advantage a matter of head size, as the sphere suggests, or of the fixed helmet's fit?* The helmet fitted at the adult's gap is the main control ([@fig-helmet-fit]). At the adult's gap, Δ is {{ F.rev_cgap_templates_scaled_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB in the scaled adults and infant templates, every template interval spanning zero: fitting the helmet reduces these heads' extra advantage to within {{ F.rev_cgap_near_zero_db }} dB of the adult's D in the same fitted construction (point estimates; interval bounds {{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_fitted_delta_ci_bounds_range }} dB, only the school-age scaled adult's just above zero). In the children, provisional examples (Section 2.2), Δ is negative in A and B ({{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} and {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} dB); in child C, whose two gaps nearly coincide ([@tab-anatomies]), it is {{ F.rev_cgap_child_c_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_c_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} dB. With the room field projected out every point estimate is below the adult's ({{ F.rev_cgap_smaller_fitted_dense_vs_combined_proj_delta_range }} dB; {{ F.wr_cgap_fitted_proj_delta_n_ci_includes_zero_words }} intervals spanning zero). The interaction, which compares each head with the adult placed by the same rule in both helmets, measures the reduction: the extra that the fixed helmet gives a smaller head beyond what it gives the adult is {{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_interaction_range }} dB in the scaled adults and templates, every interval above zero ({{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_interaction_top_range }} dB with the helmet fitted at the adult's top-contact gap). The residual depends on how the adult is placed: against the adult at top contact the heads fitted at its top-contact gap keep {{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_fittedtop_vs_top_range }} dB, and in children A and B the negative Δ is not established ({{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fittedtop_vs_fixed_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fittedtop_vs_fixed_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} and {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fittedtop_vs_fixed_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fittedtop_vs_fixed_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} dB; [S1, section E](supplement.html#s-helmets)). The templates' residuals may also be too low, because of their scalp convention (Section 2.2). Under either adult reference, most of the fixed-helmet extra is removed.

Neither decomposition below apportions the effects exactly, because the fitted helmet changes the gap distribution, the placement and the sensors' coverage together. The within-head contrast, what Neuromag loses to the fixed helmet's gap and placement (the median over a head's targets of the paired difference; [@fig-helmet-fit]d), is {{ F.rev_cgap_templates_scaled_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib_range }} dB in the scaled adults and templates, {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib }} and {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib }} dB in children A and B, {{ F.rev_cgap_child_c_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_c_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib_ci }} dB in child C and {{ F.rev_cgap_adult_fixed_minus_fitted_combined_ib }} dB in the adult, whose fitted helmet sits looser than top contact. At a fixed gap and with equal OPM site counts, Δ is {{ F.rev_cgap_smaller_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB ([S1, section E](supplement.html#s-helmets)), and in the scaled adults and templates their fewer sites take about that much back; in children A and B no such gain is established ({{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_a_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} and {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta }} {{ F.rev_cgap_child_b_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_ci }} dB). In this model, then, fitting this helmet to the adult's median gap substantially reduces the extra advantage that the scaled adults and templates show in the fixed adult helmet; it does not isolate the median gap as a cause, and the children's values remain provisional.

## 4. Discussion {#discussion}

### 4.1 Answers to the four questions {#disc-answers}

**Q1.** Across the conditions tested, the OPM noise floor or the array can reverse which system leads, and the comparator and the metric decide which reported number shows it. With sensor plus brain noise the dense array leads at every depth; the site-matched array, and the dense array after the projection or with Neuromag's measured noise (scenario D), have an equal-SNR depth (Section 3.3). The dense lead survives the omissions tested at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz but is the size of the model's error for Neuromag: until an OPM covariance is measured, the answer is a conditional prediction.

**Q2.** Under the modelled noise the dense lead is largest on the frontal and central convexity and smallest in cingulate cortex, and the site-matched array trails on ventral and polar surfaces, including mesial temporal cortex; with Neuromag's measured noise (scenario D) the frontal lead falls to about 1, the cingulate and parietal lobes fall below 1 and the temporal lobe leads (Section 3.3).

**Q3.** Fitting the helmet to the adult's median gap substantially reduces the extra advantage the smaller heads show in the fixed adult helmet: the interaction is {{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_interaction_range }} dB in the scaled adults and templates, every interval above zero. In the fixed helmet every smaller head's point estimate exceeds the adult's (child C's interval and placement band span zero); fitted at the adult's gap, the scaled adults come within {{ F.rev_cgap_scaled_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB of the adult fitted the same way and the templates within {{ F.rev_cgap_near_zero_db }} dB, a residual that depends on the adult's reference placement and, for the templates, on their scalp convention. The construction changes gap, placement and coverage together, so it does not isolate the median gap as the cause. At equal site count Δ is {{ F.rev_cgap_smaller_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB (not established in children A and B), the quantity comparable to the sensor-noise-only result of Zahran et al. (2022), and in the scaled adults and templates their fewer OPM sites take it back; these controls do not show what produces either effect. The children's values, two of them below the adult fitted the same way but not established against the adult at top contact, are provisional (Section 2.2).

**Q4.** For superficial spikes and the dense array, yes: in the confirmatory run it detected simulated spikes {{ F.cf_band }} mm deep at {{ F.cf_all_dense_vs_combined_practical_reduction_pct_range }} lower strength than Neuromag in all {{ F.wr_n_anatomies_words }} anatomies (not independent replications; fixed adult helmet; idealized detector, modelled noise), and the ranking survived the declared detector mismatch; no (exploratory) localization difference is established.

### 4.2 The clinical literature: compatibility, not reconciliation {#disc-literature}

The clinical reports and the model are compatible, but the model does not identify which factor operated in any study ([@tab-literature]). Higher or similar OPM SNR against SQUID magnetometers (Feys et al., 2022; mixed in Feys et al., 2025) corresponds to peak-channel SNR against the magnetometers, which favours the OPM in every head ([S1, section F](supplement.html#s-regions-heads)), and equal or lower SNR against planar gradiometers or the nearest channels of few or noisier sensors (Hillebrand et al., 2023; Schwartz et al., 2025) to peak-channel SNR against the gradiometers or the best channel. The larger gains reported in school-aged children in an adult helmet (Feys et al., 2022) are compatible with the fixed adult helmet's fit (gap and placement together; Sections 3.4 and 3.5). The weaker OPM results for temporal and mesial temporal sources (Feys et al., 2024, 2025; Shen et al., 2026) have two analogues, deep sources being weaker for every system and the site-matched array trailing on ventral and polar surfaces; the second is fragile, because the model overstates Neuromag's detectability in the temporal lobe (measured over modelled {{ F.rev_cov_det_lobe_temporal_corrected_ratio }}; [S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)). With a channel-averaged metric the site-matched array has a small but established lead ({{ F.g2_meanpow_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_meanpow_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci }}) where Matsubara et al. (2026) found no region with a higher OPM SNR (with brain noise only, in patients' anatomies), and a cohort's report of no difference in detection (Ren et al., 2025; abstract only) matches the site-matched array's whole-cortex detectability, not its simulated superficial-spike detection (Section 3.6).

By whole-cortex detectability against Neuromag's {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels with the oracle covariance, a single-axis array like the site-matched one ({{ F.g2_matched_sites }} sites, about the channel count of the larger clinical arrays) shows no established advantage at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz and a SQUID advantage when noisier (Section 3.1). Its small established advantages depend on the comparator, the metric, the covariance estimate or depth (within about {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_above1_to_mm }} mm of the scalp, including simulated superficial spikes in {{ F.cf_matched_vs_combined_practical_n_holm_pass }} of {{ F.meth_n_anatomies }} anatomies; Sections 3.1, 3.3 and 3.6; [@tab-literature]). Clinical arrays reach their channel counts with dual- or triaxial sensors at fewer sites; the triaxial control at the site-matched sites gives {{ F.g2_triax_equal_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}, showing what extra axes at the site-matched sites can recover; whether an array of about 64 dual-axis sites lies above or below the site-matched array, the model does not determine. The modest whole-cortex advantage belongs to an array with more sites than any used in epilepsy so far, at a white noise within the clinical range.

### 4.3 Limitations {#disc-limitations}

No measurement validates the OPM covariance: the OPM sensor noise is declared and white, and Neuromag's measured noise alone favours Neuromag by as much as the dense array's whole lead (Section 3.2). A measured OPM noise covariance in the same band would decide Q1; public OPM recordings could give a first check. A single projection stands in for the preprocessing of real systems (Taulu & Simola, 2006; Tierney et al., 2021). One adult is the reference (its D spans {{ F.g3b_adult_family_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_span }} dB over {{ F.g3b_n_family_placements }} source-blind placements), the pediatric claims rest on {{ F.wr_n_scaled_words }} copies of it, {{ F.wr_n_templates_words }} templates and {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} children of one dataset, and the parcel intervals exclude between-subject variability. The children's skull is modelled, their fiducials are transferred and unchecked on their MRIs, and the MRI check cannot settle whether their white surface is right within a few millimetres of the scalp; their negative Δ at the adult's gap may rest on that near-scalp cortex, which is why they are treated as provisional examples. The 24-month template's scalp lies {{ F.rev_qc_infant2yr_outward_mm }} mm outside its MRI head boundary, the adult's {{ F.wr_qc_adult_offset_inside_otsu_mm }} mm inside, and the other templates were not checked, so the templates' Δ may carry a difference of conventions of about {{ F.wr_qc_template_adult_convention_mm }} mm, which would make it too low; in the adult a 3-mm OPM gap lowers the dense ratio from {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} to {{ F.g2_sens_gap3mm_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} (Section 3.1). The within-head contrast and the interaction, which compare two helmets on one scalp, are less exposed to it. The smaller heads keep the adult's background variance per unit cortical area; their fixed-helmet gain stays positive when it is multiplied by {{ F.g3b_cfg_bg_factor_low }} or {{ F.g3b_cfg_bg_factor_high }} ([S4](pediatric.html)). All sources lie on the cortical surface, so hippocampus and amygdala are absent. The spike simulations are idealized: the detector's templates, candidate patterns and whitening covariance come from the simulation's own stationary model, the null data hold no physiological transients, and the ranking was tested against one declared mismatch under the primary noise model only. Head motion was bounded and is not part of the spike simulations ([S1, section G](supplement.html#s-spikes)).

### 4.4 Conclusions {#conclusions}

Across the conditions tested, the OPM noise floor or the array can reverse which system leads, while source depth changes the size of the difference, and the comparator and the metric decide whether a reported SNR shows it. The modest lead of a dense array is a conditional prediction until an OPM noise covariance is measured; a single-axis array at Neuromag's {{ F.g2_matched_sites }} sites, about the channel count of the larger clinical arrays, shows no established whole-cortex advantage with the oracle covariance at the primary noise level (a small one, {{ F.g2_plugin60s_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_plugin60s_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci }}, with a covariance estimated from {{ F.g2_plugin60s_samples }} samples) and a SQUID advantage when noisier. The dense lead is smallest in cingulate cortex and the site-matched array trails on ventral and polar surfaces (exploratory rankings). Fitting the helmet to the adult's median gap removes most of the extra advantage that the scaled adults and templates show in the fixed adult helmet (an interaction of {{ F.wr_cgap_scaled_templates_interaction_range }} dB); how much remains depends on the adult's reference placement, and the children are provisional examples. A confirmatory run (idealized detector, modelled noise, fixed adult helmet) found a dense array detecting simulated superficial spikes at a lower strength than Neuromag in all {{ F.wr_n_anatomies_words }} anatomies (not independent replications); no (exploratory) localization difference is established. Clinical reports of higher OPM SNR are compatible with these conditions, which the model cannot tell apart in any study.
