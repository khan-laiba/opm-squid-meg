**Abstract**

On-scalp optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs) record larger fields than cryogenic SQUID magnetoencephalography (MEG), but a spherical model predicts an OPM advantage only above an equal-SNR depth set by an assumed noise ratio. We replaced that ratio with explicit sensor, brain and room noise, comparing the {{ F.g2_squid_channels }}-channel Neuromag system with a dense ({{ F.g2_dense_sites }}-site) and a site-matched ({{ F.g2_matched_sites }}-site) OPM array by known-topography detectability in one adult (the MNE sample subject) and {{ F.wr_n_smaller_heads_words }} smaller heads ({{ F.wr_n_scaled_words }} scaled copies of it, {{ F.wr_n_templates_words }} infant templates and {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} school-aged children). With sensor and brain noise a dense {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }}-fT/√Hz array led by {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}, falling to {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz; the site-matched array showed no established advantage ({{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci }}) and trailed Neuromag after projection ({{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_proj_ratio }}). The dense lead survived the model's known omissions but is the size of its error for Neuromag: with Neuromag's measured noise covariance the implied ratio ranges from {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s3_ratio }} to {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s2_ratio }}, {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ci }} with the OPM as modelled. The dense lead was smallest in cingulate cortex, the site-matched array weakest on ventral and polar surfaces. In the fixed adult helmet at top contact the smaller heads' lead, D in dB, exceeded the adult's by {{ F.g3b_smaller_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB (one child's interval spanning zero); in a helmet fitted at the adult's gap it was {{ F.rev_cgap_templates_scaled_fitted_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB in the scaled adults and templates and negative in {{ F.wr_n_children_below_adult_fitted_words }} of {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} children. No localization difference was established. In this model and these anatomies the noise floor and the array, more than depth, decide the comparison; the fixed helmet's gap, not head size, gives smaller heads their larger advantage; the clinical reports are compatible with, not explained by, these conditions.

## 1. Introduction {#intro}

Optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs) measure the brain's magnetic field at room temperature and can be worn on the scalp, whereas the superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs) of conventional magnetoencephalography (MEG) sit in a rigid cryogenic helmet at a distance set by its Dewar (Boto et al., 2017; Iivanainen et al., 2017). The helmet is built for adults: a child's head sits in it with a wide and uneven gap, whereas an on-scalp array fits any head (Hill et al., 2019; Feys & De Tiège, 2024). Clinical OPM studies of epilepsy report larger interictal discharges than cryogenic MEG (Feys et al., 2022), signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) higher in some comparisons (Feys et al., 2022, 2025; Ren et al., 2025, abstract only) and equal or slightly lower in others (Hillebrand et al., 2023; Schwartz et al., 2025), and the weakest OPM results for temporal and mesial temporal sources (Feys et al., 2024, 2025; Shen et al., 2026).

A larger field does not by itself give a higher SNR. Jas et al. (2026) showed this in a spherical head with a point magnetometer on the scalp (OPM) and {{ F.lit_xi_squid_mm }} mm above it (SQUID): if the OPM's noise is η times the SQUID's, its single-channel SNR is higher only for sources shallower than an equal-SNR depth, about {{ F.lit_deq_eta3_printed_mm }} mm at η = {{ F.lit_eta_reference }} in an adult. With a SQUID shell that follows the head at a constant standoff, that depth covers a larger fraction of a small brain, a predicted benefit for children.

A realistic comparison needs explicit noise of each kind seen through each array's own lead fields, finite arrays on a real scalp and a real helmet around a smaller head. With sensor noise only, a helium-4 OPM array's advantage over SQUID magnetometers grew as the head shrank, in a standard adult placement and in a SQUID helmet fitted around each head at a constant gap alike (Zahran et al., 2022). With brain noise on one adult, simulated spikes were visible on the scalp from smaller cortical patches than with conventional MEG (Westin et al., 2023), whereas OPMs at the SQUID positions raised a channel-averaged SNR in no region (Matsubara et al., 2026). None combined brain noise with a real adult helmet around a smaller head or separated head size from helmet fit at the adult's own gap; here the noise model is also checked against a measured Neuromag covariance.

We therefore ask four questions. (Q1) What decides the comparison between OPM arrays and the Neuromag system on a real adult head with explicit noise, and how far can the noise model be trusted? (Q2) Which brain regions favour the OPM, and where does an array at the SQUID sites fall behind? (Q3) Is the larger relative advantage expected in smaller heads a matter of head size or of the fixed adult helmet? (Q4) Does a detectability difference change which simulated interictal spikes are detected, and how well they are localized?

## 2. Methods {#methods}

### 2.1 Study design and estimands {#meth-design}

The primary metric is known-topography detectability, d = √(sᵀC⁻¹s): the output SNR of the matched filter for a source of known field pattern s in noise of covariance C, with the true (oracle) covariance. Arrays are compared target by target by the paired ratio d~OPM~/d~Neuromag~, or by D = 20 log~10~ of it in dB, positive when the OPM is ahead. For the adult we report the unweighted median over all {{ F.g2_n_targets }} targets, the {{ F.g2_medialwall_n }} on the medial wall included, as a ratio. For the smaller heads, whose cortices differ in size, we report D as an area-weighted median without the medial wall, and Δ = D~head~ − D~adult~ with the adult placed by the same helmet rule. At the adult's measured head position the dense array's ratio is {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} (D = {{ F.wr_adult_dense_ib_db }} dB) under the first convention and {{ F.wr_adult_dense_ib_ratio_area_weighted_cortical_3dp }} ({{ F.g3b_link_g2_db }} dB) under the second ([@tab-estimands]).

The {{ F.wr_n_smaller_heads_words }} smaller heads fall into three classes ([@tab-anatomies]): {{ F.wr_n_scaled_words }} **scaled adults**, copies of the adult scaled to school-age size and to the head circumference of the {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}-month template; {{ F.wr_n_templates_words }} **infant templates** at {{ F.g3b_infant2yr_age_months }}, {{ F.g3b_infant18mo_age_months }} and {{ F.g3b_infant12mo_age_months }} months, averages of many MRIs (O'Reilly et al., 2021; Richards et al., 2016); and {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} **school-aged children**, A, B and C ({{ F.g3b_children_age_range }} years), typically developing children (OpenNeuro ds005234; Fadeev et al., 2024, 2025). ### 2.3 Arrays and helmets {#meth-arrays}

**Neuromag (306 channels)** combines the sample recording's VectorView geometry, MNE-Python's coil definitions for the TRIUX sensors and the TRIUX datasheet's typical white noise, {{ F.meth_squid_mag_asd }} fT/√Hz and {{ F.meth_squid_grad_asd }} fT/(cm √Hz), with its {{ F.meth_dewar_mm }}-mm Dewar spacing between coils and helmet (Elekta Neuromag, n.d.); no signal-space separation is applied.

**OPM arrays** ([@fig-arrays]a to c). Each OPM is a single-axis sensor with its sensing centre {{ F.g2_dense_standoff_mm }} mm above the MRI scalp (Osborne et al., 2018; Alem et al., 2023) and a {{ F.g2_opm_cell_mm }}-mm cubic averaging volume, a modelling choice that changes the peak field by at most {{ F.wr_opm_cell_peak_effect_pct }} ([S7](register.html)). Its axis is the head-surface normal averaged within {{ F.meth_opm_axis_radius_mm }} mm of the site (nearly radial axes give a projected dense ratio of {{ F.meth_hse_radial_dense_proj_ratio_3dp }} instead of {{ F.meth_hse_final_dense_proj_ratio_3dp }}). Above the preauricular plane, the **dense OPM array** fills the scalp at a minimum spacing of {{ F.g2_dense_packing_mm }} mm ({{ F.g2_dense_sites }} sites on the adult, more than any clinical OPM array; Shen et al., 2026), and the **site-matched OPM array** puts one OPM at each Neuromag site inside the region, projected onto the scalp ({{ F.g2_matched_sites }} sites). Both are refitted to every head ([@tab-anatomies]); a triaxial array at the matched sites controls for channel count. The OPM white noise is {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz in the primary analysis, flat over {{ F.g2_band_range_hz }} Hz, and is swept over {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep }} fT/√Hz (the clinical studies state no band for their noise figures; the device specifications behind them begin at 1 Hz or above, e.g. Osborne et al., 2018; [S7](register.html)).

The **fixed adult helmet** ([@fig-arrays]d) keeps the adult's sensors and noise for every head. The primary placement is *top contact*: from the pose in which the head's frame lies where the adult's does in the recording, the head is raised until the nearest magnetometer coil is {{ F.g3b_cfg_clearance }} mm from the scalp, a best-fit assumption that favours Neuromag. A family of {{ F.g3b_n_family_placements }} source-blind placements, chosen from the geometry alone (contacts, shifts and rotations; {{ F.g3b_child_c_n_family_feasible }} feasible in child C; [S1, section E](supplement.html#s-helmets)), gives the placement band.

**Helmet fitted at the adult's gap.** Each head is *laterally centred* (moved sideways to the helmet's midline), and the helmet is scaled about the head origin until the median magnetometer-to-scalp gap equals the adult's in the same construction, {{ F.rev_cgap_adult_gap_mm }} mm, with no coil within the {{ F.g3b_cfg_dewar_spacing }}-mm Dewar spacing of the scalp; in child C that limit binds first, at {{ F.rev_cgap_child_c_fitted_gap_mm }} mm. The **helmet scaled with the head**, which scales the gap too, is a secondary construction ([S1, section E](supplement.html#s-helmets)), and an **equal-site-count control** subsamples the adult's dense array to each smaller head's site count. None models a pediatric SQUID device.

### 2.4 Noise model, validation and sensitivity analyses {#meth-noise}

Every array sees identical noise sources in one band of {{ F.g2_band_range_hz }} Hz. Each array's oracle covariance is computed in closed form as C = Σ~s~ + s² L~bg~ A L~bg~ᵀ + E Σ~e~ Eᵀ, where Σ~s~ is the diagonal white sensor noise, L~bg~ holds the lead fields of {{ F.g2_n_background }} independent cortex-normal background dipoles on a {{ F.g2_bg_grid_mm }}-mm grid, A is the diagonal matrix of their Voronoi-cell areas, and E and Σ~e~ are the array's responses to {{ F.g2_room_terms }} external-field terms and their coefficient covariance. Each background dipole's moment variance is s² times its area (s² = {{ F.meth_bg_var_per_mm2_nam2 }} nAm² per mm²); like the targets, the background omits the cortex within {{ F.g2_source_skull_clearance_mm }} mm of the inner skull. Two quantities are fitted to the sample dataset: Σ~e~ (to the empty-room recording) and the scalar s², so that the median good gradiometer's modelled brain noise equals the measured one (an RMS of {{ F.meth_bg_calibration_grad_ft_cm }} fT/cm). Everything else is a prediction, except the declared sensor noise of both systems. The conditions are sensor noise only; sensor plus brain noise (primary); with the room field; and projected, the room field's subspace removed from every array by the same noise-weighted projection.

**Validation.** The model's Neuromag covariance was compared with the measured one of the same recording, and Neuromag's detectability was recomputed with its measured covariance (finite-sample corrected; [S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)); as the OPM's covariance cannot be measured here, the implied OPM/Neuromag ratio was computed under five stated assumptions, scenarios A to E (Section 3.2).

**Sensitivity analyses**, each changing one element of the adult model and recalibrating s²: cortex {{ F.rev_ns_joint_floor_mm }} to {{ F.rev_ns_near_rule_mm }} mm from the inner skull in the background, as targets, or both; coloured OPM noise (1/f plus white, corners at {{ F.rev_ns_col_corners_hz }} Hz) under a matched filter that also whitens across frequency; idealized cardiac and ocular far-field sources (also added on top of the calibrated background); and all together. The spike simulations use the primary noise model only.

### 2.5 Simulated interictal spikes {#meth-spikes}

The spike study runs the noise model in the time domain, in segments shared by all arrays, with the spike-wave complex of Hunold et al. (2016) stretched to {{ F.wr_n_morphologies_words }} morphologies as the spike. Two matched-filter detectors, whitened with a Ledoit–Wolf covariance (Ledoit & Wolf, 2004), read the data: the oracle knows source and time; the practical detector knows neither, scans candidate patterns on a {{ F.g4_dictionary_spacing_mm }}-mm grid without the true sources, and has thresholds for {{ F.g4_operating_point_per_minute }} false event per minute frozen on independent null data. Both are idealized (Section 4.3).

**Exploratory design.** Spikes were placed at {{ F.g4_n_locations }} locations stratified by depth ({{ F.g4_n_depth_bands }} bands from {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} to {{ F.g4_band_45_70mm }} mm, {{ F.g4_locations_per_band }} locations each) and orientation, each with {{ F.g4_n_focal_events_per_location }} focal events of {{ F.g4_strengths_nam }} nAm and patch events, in one noise realization per event, identical in every array. The paired S50 ratio ([@tab-estimands]) is above 1 when the OPM needs less strength. The adult sits at its measured head position here and the smaller heads at top contact; every spike comparison is within one anatomy.

**Confirmatory design, fixed before the run.** The endpoint is the dense OPM array against Neuromag (306 channels) with the practical detector at {{ F.g4_operating_point_per_minute }} false event per minute, for focal spikes {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm below the scalp, in all {{ F.meth_n_anatomies }} anatomies, tested by the two-sided sign-flip test on per-location differences in detection counts, Holm-corrected over the anatomies (Holm, 1979) at α = {{ F.meth_alpha }}, with the paired S50 ratio as the effect size. After {{ F.wr_confirm_pilot_runs_words }} underpowered pilot runs with {{ F.wr_confirm_pilot_locs_per_band }} locations ([S1, section G](supplement.html#s-spikes)), the run draws {{ F.meth_confirm_locs }} new locations per anatomy in the {{ F.g4_band_10_20mm }} mm band ({{ F.meth_confirm_locs_per_stratum }} per orientation stratum), gives every event {{ F.wr_confirm_replicates_words }} noise replicates, the first confirmatory and the others a measure of Monte Carlo variability, with separate null data for threshold fitting and rate evaluation. One declared detector-mismatch variant (off-template morphologies, a single-compartment BEM, a shared coregistration error of {{ F.wr_confirm_coreg_shift_mm }} mm and {{ F.wr_confirm_coreg_angle_deg }}°), the site-matched array, the oracle and rate-matched thresholds are secondary.

**Localization**, in the exploratory run, used dipole fits (ECD) and dynamic statistical parametric mapping (dSPM; Dale et al., 2000) at the true peak sample on {{ F.loc_n_locations }} locations, through a single-compartment BEM with a coregistration error of {{ F.loc_coreg_shift_mm }} mm and {{ F.loc_coreg_angle_deg }}° (tests in [S1, section G](supplement.html#s-spikes)).

### 2.6 Statistics {#meth-stats}

Neighbouring targets are correlated, so intervals are {{ F.g2_ci_level_pct }} percentile bootstrap intervals over the adult's {{ F.g2_n_parcels }} atlas labels or over each smaller head's parcels (resample counts: [@stab-resamples]); they describe spatial variation within one anatomy only. In the spike study the location is the unit: S50 ratios carry location-bootstrap intervals, and detection differences are tested with the sign-flip test. The exploratory spike endpoint (chosen from {{ F.g4_family_per_opm_array }} comparisons per OPM array), the primary helmet placement and the regions were named after the whole-cortex results were known, and the model was revised while results were known ([S1, section H](supplement.html#s-history)); only the confirmatory run carries a pre-specified test.

### 2.7 Software, data and code {#meth-software}

Simulations used MNE-Python {{ F.g3b_mne_version }} (Gramfort et al., 2013) and NumPy {{ F.g3b_numpy_version }}, with the seeds listed in [S1, section I](supplement.html#s-tables). The code, configurations and tests are public at [github.com/khan-laiba/opm-squid-meg](https://github.com/khan-laiba/opm-squid-meg) (MIT licence; the sphere benchmark's legacy dependency included); this report, its figures and the result files are released under CC BY 4.0 ([S8](numbers.html), [S9](reproduce.html)).

## 3. Results {#results}

### 3.1 What decides the adult comparison {#res-conditions}

*With explicit noise, which array detects a cortical source better, and what decides it?* With sensor noise alone, Neuromag leads both OPM arrays: the median paired ratio over the {{ F.g2_n_targets }} targets is {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_int_ratio }} for the dense array and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_int_ratio }} for the site-matched array ([@fig-conditions]). Adding the cortical background reverses this. With the room field included, brain noise is {{ F.g2_noise_share_dense_brain_pct }} of a dense-array OPM channel's in-band variance and {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_brain_pct }} of a Neuromag magnetometer's (room field {{ F.g2_noise_share_squid_mag_room_pct }}), so the OPMs' higher sensor noise no longer decides. With sensor plus brain noise the dense array's ratio is {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}, D = {{ F.wr_adult_dense_ib_db }} dB, and the OPM is ahead at all but {{ F.rev_g2_notahead_n }} targets. The site-matched array's ratio is {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}, D = {{ F.wr_adult_matched_ib_db }} dB: no established advantage.

The **comparator**: against the {{ F.g2_squid_grad_channels }} gradiometers alone the ratios are {{ F.g2_dense_vs_grad_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_grad_ib_ratio }} (dense, site-matched), against the {{ F.g2_squid_sites }} magnetometers {{ F.g2_dense_vs_mag_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_mag_ib_ratio }}. The **metric**: by peak-channel SNR, the closest analogue of clinical sensor-level SNRs, the dense array is ahead of the magnetometers ({{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_mag_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_mag_ib_ci }}) but behind the best of all {{ F.g2_squid_channels }} channels ({{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}), and by mean-power SNR its lead is small ({{ F.g2_meanpow_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). The **OPM noise floor**: from {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_min }} to {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz the dense ratio falls from {{ F.g2_sens_asd7ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} to {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}, no established advantage, and the site-matched ratio from {{ F.g2_sens_asd7ft_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} to {{ F.g2_sens_asd30ft_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }}; the break-even white noise is {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_ib_asd }} {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_ib_asd_ci }} fT/√Hz for the dense array and {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_matched_vs_combined_ib_asd }} {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_matched_vs_combined_ib_asd_ci }} fT/√Hz for the site-matched array ([@fig-noise-checks]d).

Other choices move the dense ratio less: projecting out the room field gives {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_dense_vs_combined_proj_ci_3dp }} and {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_proj_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_proj_ci_3dp }}, the site-matched array then behind Neuromag; a {{ F.g2_opm_gap_small_mm }}-mm scalp gap lowers the dense ratio to {{ F.g2_sens_gap3mm_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}, and {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz with that gap reverses the ranking ({{ F.g2_joint_gap3mm_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.g2_joint_gap3mm_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }}); a triaxial array at the matched sites recovers much of the dense lead ({{ F.g2_triax_equal_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}; {{ F.g2_triax_tang2x_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} with the tangential axes twice as noisy); a plug-in covariance from a short baseline raises it ({{ F.g2_plugin10s_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} from {{ F.g2_plugin10s_samples }} samples); the other rows and bands move it little. In this model, then, the noise floor, array, comparator and metric decide which system leads, and depth sets the size of the lead (Section 3.3).

### 3.2 How far the noise model can be trusted {#res-noise}

*What does the model get right about Neuromag's noise, and what does that imply for the OPM?* Only the room-field covariance and one scalar of the cortical background are fitted ([@sfig-noise-budget]). The magnetometers' brain-noise amplitude, which the calibration does not touch, comes out at {{ F.rev_cov_brain_mag_amp_ratio }} of the measured value ({{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_amp_without_ratio }} without the heart's field, which is {{ F.rev_cov_heart_mag_share_shortfall_pct }} of the shortfall), the prediction falling to {{ F.rev_cov_band_b30_40_mag_amp_ratio }} at {{ F.rev_cov_band_b30_40_label }} Hz. The gradiometer level at randomly held-out sites ({{ F.rev_cov_heldout_random_grad_amp_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_random_grad_amp_ci }}) and the overall pattern of channel correlations agree, but the modelled brain noise is too flat from bottom to top ({{ F.rev_cov_heldout_inf_to_sup_grad_amp_ratio }} and {{ F.rev_cov_heldout_sup_to_inf_grad_amp_ratio }} for the directional halves), its magnetometer correlations fall off too fast with distance, the per-channel variances are poorly predicted, and the magnetometers' excess over the model sits in a few far-field components ([S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)). For detectability this excess favours Neuromag: with its measured covariance Neuromag's detectability is {{ F.rev_cov_det_combined_corrected_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_det_combined_corrected_ci }} times its modelled value ([@fig-noise-checks]a).

What this implies for the OPM depends on its real noise, which no measurement here constrains ([@fig-noise-checks]b). If the model's error is common to both systems (scenario A), the dense ratio stays at {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s1_ratio }} and the site-matched at {{ F.rev_cov_opm_matched_vs_combined_s1_ratio }}, and nearly so under scenarios B and E ([S1, section C](supplement.html#s-noise)). If Neuromag is taken as measured and the OPM as modelled (scenario D), the dense ratio is {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ratio }} {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s4_ci }}, no established advantage, and the site-matched {{ F.rev_cov_opm_matched_vs_combined_s4_ratio }}; if the OPM bears the whole magnetometer variance excess as cortical noise (scenario C, a pessimistic bound), they are {{ F.rev_cov_opm_dense_vs_combined_s3_ratio }} and {{ F.rev_cov_opm_matched_vs_combined_s3_ratio }}.

The model's omissions move the lead little at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz ([@fig-noise-checks]c). Cortex {{ F.rev_ns_joint_floor_mm }} to {{ F.rev_ns_near_rule_mm }} mm from the inner skull gives {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_bg_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} added to the background, {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_targets_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} as targets and {{ F.rev_ns_near_primary_f2mm_both_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} as both. A 1/f component cornering at {{ F.rev_ns_joint_corner_hz }} Hz, under a detector that also whitens across frequency, gives {{ F.rev_ns_col_fr_asd15ft_c10hz_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} ({{ F.rev_ns_col_fr_asd15ft_c0hz_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} with white noise under the same detector). Cardiac and ocular sources change the dense ratio by {{ F.rev_ns_ff_dense_vs_combined_ib_change_pct_range }} with sensor plus brain noise and by {{ F.rev_ns_ff_dense_vs_combined_proj_change_pct_range }} after the projection. The most adverse combination, near-skull background with the {{ F.rev_ns_joint_corner_hz }}-Hz corner ([@fig-noise-checks]d, dashed), gives {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd15ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd15ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }}, {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd20ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd20ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci_3dp }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_20 }} and {{ F.rev_ns_joint_nofar_asd30ft_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio_3dp }} at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_max }} fT/√Hz ([@stab-breakeven] gives every alternative): under it the lead is established only up to somewhere between {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} and {{ F.g2_opm_asd_sweep_20 }} fT/√Hz, against the primary model's break-even of {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_ib_asd }} fT/√Hz ({{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_proj_asd }} {{ F.rev_ns_breakeven_dense_vs_combined_proj_asd_ci }} after the projection). A measured OPM covariance is the missing test.

### 3.3 Depth and regions {#res-regions}

*Where on the cortex does the dense array lead, and where does the site-matched array fall behind?* The lead is largest near the scalp ([@fig-adult-depth]a): the dense ratio is {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_10_15_ratio }} {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_10_15_ci }} in the {{ F.g2_depth_bin_10_15_mm }} mm bin, {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_25_30_ratio }} {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_ib_25_30_ci }} at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_25_30_mm }} mm and {{ F.g2_depth_dense_vs_combined_ib_35_60_range }} from {{ F.g2_depth_bin_35_40_mm }} to {{ F.g2_depth_bin_55_60_mm }} mm, every interval above 1. The site-matched array, in contrast, has an equal-SNR depth even with sensor plus brain noise: its intervals lie above 1 down to the {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_above1_through_bin }} mm bin and below 1 from {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci_below1_from_bin }} mm, so its overall median of {{ F.g2_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} combines a superficial lead with a deep deficit. With the room field projected out, by the median, the dense median is below 1 from {{ F.rev_db_dense_vs_combined_proj_median_below1_from_bin }} mm (its interval only at {{ F.g2_depth_bin_55_60_mm }} mm) and the site-matched from {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_proj_median_below1_from_bin }} mm (interval from {{ F.rev_db_matched_vs_combined_proj_ci_below1_from_bin }} mm). For the dense array the fall is geometric: it is present with sensor noise alone ([@fig-adult-depth]a, dotted) and in the peak field itself ([@fig-adult-depth]b), brain noise multiplying the ratio by a nearly constant factor in every bin ({{ F.g2_brainmult_dense_vs_combined_range }}). On the cortex ([@fig-adult-depth]c) the dense array is ahead almost everywhere, most on the crowns of the lateral convexity, the site-matched array behind mainly on ventral and polar surfaces.

By region ([@fig-regions-adult]) the dense lead is largest in the frontal lobe ({{ F.g2_lobe_frontal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_lobe_frontal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}) and smallest in the cingulate ({{ F.g2_lobe_cingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_lobe_cingulate_dense_vs_combined_ib_ci }}) and the insula ({{ F.g2_lobe_insula_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). Of the regions chosen from the epilepsy literature, the precentral gyrus favours it most ({{ F.reg_precentral_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}) and the parahippocampal cortex least ({{ F.reg_parahippocampal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}; with the entorhinal cortex, the mesial temporal group that stands proxy for mesial temporal generators, {{ F.reg_mesial_temporal_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), though {{ F.reg_dk_dense_vs_combined_ib_below_parahippocampal_count }} atlas parcels, mostly cingulate and medial, have smaller leads. The site-matched array trails most on ventral and polar surfaces: the frontal pole ({{ F.reg_dk_frontalpole_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), medial and lateral orbitofrontal cortex ({{ F.reg_dk_medialorbitofrontal_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.reg_dk_lateralorbitofrontal_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), pars orbitalis and the temporal pole ({{ F.reg_dk_parsorbitalis_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.reg_dk_temporalpole_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}), and mesial temporal cortex ({{ F.reg_mesial_temporal_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). These rankings are exploratory point estimates of the model, conditional on the OPM's unmeasured noise: Neuromag's measured noise departs from the modelled one by lobe, and under scenario D (Section 3.2) the dense ratio becomes {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_cingulate_ratio }} in the cingulate, {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_frontal_ratio }} in the frontal and {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_lobe_temporal_ratio }} in the temporal lobe, and falls below 1 from the {{ F.wr_cov_s4_dense_depth_first_below1_label }} mm bin (point estimates, room field included).

### 3.4 Smaller heads in the fixed adult helmet {#res-children}

*Does a smaller head in the adult helmet change the OPM's relative performance?* At top contact the median magnetometer-to-scalp gap is {{ F.g3b_smaller_top_gap_range }} mm in the smaller heads against {{ F.g3b_adult_top_gap }} mm in the adult ([@tab-anatomies]). ## 4. Discussion {#discussion}

### 4.1 Answers to the four questions {#disc-answers}

**Q1.** With explicit noise, the OPM noise floor, the array, the comparator and the metric decide the adult comparison; depth sets the size of the lead, and an equal-SNR depth exists only for some arrays and conditions (none for the dense array with the modelled sensor and brain noise, one for the site-matched array). The dense lead survives the model's known omissions but is the size of the model's error for Neuromag: until an OPM covariance is measured, the answer is a conditional prediction.

**Q2.** The dense lead is largest on the frontal and central convexity and smallest in cingulate cortex. The site-matched array trails on ventral and polar surfaces, most on the frontal pole and orbitofrontal cortex, and in mesial temporal cortex. The ranking holds under the modelled noise; with Neuromag's measured noise the cingulate and frontal leads shrink and the temporal lead grows (Section 3.3).

**Q3.** With sensor and brain noise every smaller head's point estimate of the dense-array advantage exceeds the adult's in the fixed adult helmet (child C's interval and placement band span zero). In a helmet fitted at the adult's gap the scaled adults and infant templates come within {{ F.rev_cgap_near_zero_db }} dB of the adult's D (point estimates; every template interval spans zero), {{ F.wr_n_children_below_adult_fitted_words }} of the {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} children fall below it, and with the room field projected out every smaller head's point estimate is below the adult's. With sensor noise only, Zahran et al. (2022) found the OPM advantage growing as the head shrank at a constant gap; here, with brain noise, that route is small ({{ F.rev_cgap_smaller_fitted_eqsites_dense_vs_combined_ib_delta_range }} dB at equal site count) and is taken back by fewer sites and, in the children, by brain noise from cortex close to the OPMs. In this model and these anatomies, the fixed helmet's gap, not head size, gives smaller heads their larger OPM advantage.

<!-- B3: spike sentence (Q4: the first sentence of the paragraph below) -->

**Q4.** No localization difference is established; dipole errors are of the order of the coregistration displacement and the inverse model's mismatch in every array.

### 4.2 The clinical literature: compatibility, not reconciliation {#disc-literature}

The clinical reports and the model are compatible, but the model does not identify which factor operated in a given study ([@tab-literature]), and the comparison is one of direction. Higher or similar OPM SNR against SQUID magnetometers (Feys et al., 2022; mixed in Feys et al., 2025) corresponds to peak-channel SNR against the magnetometers, which favours the OPM in the adult (dense {{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_mag_ib_ratio }}, site-matched {{ F.g2_peak_matched_vs_mag_ib_ratio }}) and in the children. Equal or lower SNR against planar gradiometers or the nearest channels of few or noisier sensors (Hillebrand et al., 2023; Schwartz et al., 2025) corresponds to peak-channel SNR against the gradiometers or the best channel ({{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_grad_ib_ratio }} and {{ F.g2_peak_dense_vs_combined_ib_ratio }}). The larger gains reported in school-aged children in an adult helmet (Feys et al., 2022) are compatible with the helmet gap of Section 3.5. The weaker OPM results for temporal and mesial temporal sources, within one system (Feys et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2026) or relative to magnetometers (Feys et al., 2025), have their analogues in deep sources being weaker for every system and in the ventral and polar surfaces on which the site-matched array trails, an analogue that rests on the lobe where Neuromag's measured noise departs most from the model (Section 3.3). With a channel-averaged metric the site-matched array's lead is small ({{ F.g2_meanpow_matched_vs_combined_ib_ratio }} {{ F.g2_meanpow_matched_vs_combined_ib_ci }}), where Matsubara et al. (2026) found no region with a higher OPM SNR, and a cohort's report of no significant difference in detection (Ren et al., 2025; abstract only) is consistent with the site-matched array's detectability.

For single-axis arrays like the site-matched one ({{ F.g2_matched_sites }} sites, about the channel count of the larger clinical arrays) at {{ F.g2_opm_asd_primary }} fT/√Hz or noisier, this model gives no established advantage or a SQUID advantage (Section 3.1). The modest OPM advantage belongs to an array denser than any used in epilepsy so far, at a white noise within the range reported for clinical sensors.

### 4.3 Limitations {#disc-limitations}

No measurement validates the OPM covariance: the OPM sensor noise is declared and white, and the Neuromag validation shows that one system's real noise can favour it by as much as the dense array's whole lead. A single projection stands in for the preprocessing of real systems (Taulu & Simola, 2006; Tierney et al., 2021). There is one adult reference, whose D spans {{ F.g3b_adult_family_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_span }} dB over the {{ F.g3b_n_family_placements }} source-blind placements; the pediatric claims rest on {{ F.wr_n_scaled_words }} copies of it, {{ F.wr_n_templates_words }} templates and {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} children of one dataset, and the parcel intervals exclude all variability between subjects. The children's skull is modelled, their fiducials are transferred and unchecked on their MRIs, and the MRI check cannot settle whether their white surface is right within a few millimetres of the scalp; their negative Δ at the adult's gap rests on that near-scalp cortex. The smaller heads keep the adult's background per unit cortical area, an assumption; halving or doubling it leaves their fixed-helmet difference from the adult positive ({{ F.g3b_smaller_bg0p5_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_range }} and {{ F.g3b_smaller_bg2_dense_vs_combined_ib_d_minus_adult_range }} dB). All sources lie on the cortical surface, so hippocampus and amygdala are absent. The spike stage is an idealized scanner experiment: templates, candidates and whitening covariance come from the simulation's own stationary model (no covariance mismatch), the null data hold no physiological transients, and the spikes were simulated under the primary noise model only, so their ranking is tested against the declared mismatch variant alone. Head motion was bounded, not simulated in time ([S1, section G](supplement.html#s-spikes); page [S5](epilepsy.html)).

### 4.4 Conclusions {#conclusions}

In this model, whether an on-scalp array outperforms the SQUID system depends on the OPM noise floor, the array and the comparator more than on source depth, and the modest lead of a dense array is a conditional prediction until an OPM noise covariance is measured. For single-axis arrays at about the site count of the larger clinical arrays, at the model's primary noise level or noisier, the model gives no established advantage or a SQUID advantage. In the fixed adult helmet, smaller heads gain more than the adult (not established in one child), and in this model and these anatomies the helmet's gap, not head size, gives them that gain: at the adult's gap their advantage comes back to about the adult's in the scaled adults and templates and falls below it in {{ F.wr_n_children_below_adult_fitted_words }} of {{ F.wr_n_children_words }} children. No localization difference is established. The clinical reports of higher OPM SNR are compatible with these conditions through the helmet gap and through peak-channel metrics against magnetometers, which the model cannot tell apart in any one study.

This study used only publicly available, de-identified data collected under their original studies' approvals; no new data were collected.
