Project summary by milestone

Milestone report. This page presents the analyses as they were first run and reported. The manuscript and its supplementary text (S1) give the current interpretation, the conditions of each result and the analyses added in the revision (among them the check of the noise model against the measured covariance, the helmet fitted at the adult's gap, the school-aged children as provisional examples and the confirmatory spike run), and they take precedence where this page differs.

Simulation study comparing on-scalp optically pumped magnetometers (OPM) with the Neuromag SQUID system: an analytical benchmark and adaptations of two published adult studies, a realistic adult comparison, a pediatric fixed-helmet versus head-adaptive extension, and interictal-spike detection and localization examples in both. The OPM advantage is tested, not assumed. Labels: REPRO reproduction with the paper's definitions, ADAPT adaptation where data or details are unavailable, NEW new experiment.

Adult findings, with their conditions

With modelled brain noise, the dense 208-site OPM array has 1.14x [1.12-1.17] the known-topography detectability of the Neuromag system (all 306 channels; median over 7,661 targets, the 558 on the medial wall included, 95 % CI from a bootstrap over cortical parcels; higher at all but 4 targets). An OPM array at Neuromag's own sites (98 of 102 fit) shows no established advantage: 1.01x [0.99-1.03]. Conditions: OPM white noise 15 fT/√Hz, 3-layer BEM, no extra scalp gap, the sample subject's measured head position; the modelled brain noise predicts 0.73x the measured magnetometer level.
The advantage depends on the assumptions: OPM noise 7 or 30 fT/√Hz gives 1.30x or 1.01x; with 30 fT/√Hz and a 6-mm scalp gap together, 0.92x; a 1-layer head model gives 1.13x. Without brain noise (intrinsic sensor noise only) Neuromag wins: 0.74x [0.72-0.77].
By depth (dense vs Neuromag): 1.60x at 10-15 mm below the scalp, 1.08x at 30-35 mm and 1.06x at 50-55 mm; the matched array goes from 1.20x near the scalp to 0.94x at its lowest depth bin.
Simulated interictal spikes, exploratory run (the confirmatory run is in the report, Section 3.6), practical detector at a nominal 1 false event per minute: 50 % detection needs 34 nAm with the dense OPM array vs 47 nAm with Neuromag at 10-20 mm depth (Neuromag / dense strength ratio and paired location-bootstrap 95 % interval 1.36 [1.10-1.58]). Locations with more events detected only by the dense array / only by Neuromag (exact sign-flip p, uncorrected): 10-20 mm 11/1 (p = 0.0039); 20-30 mm 9/1 (p = 0.014); 30-45 mm 5/1 (p = 0.19); 45-70 mm 9/0 (p = 0.0039). Matched array: 10-20 mm 7/4 (p = 0.4); 20-30 mm 4/5 (p = 1); 30-45 mm 2/5 (p = 0.77); 45-70 mm 3/6 (p = 0.4).
Bounded localization (24 locations, one event each): for detected 320-nAm focal spikes the dipole error on the MRI is 3.7, 5.8 and 4.2 mm (Neuromag, matched, dense), limited by a 2-mm/2-deg coregistration error in every inverse. For 320-nAm patches the dSPM error changes by -2.4 mm (matched, p = 0.067) and -5.6 mm (dense, p = 0.054) relative to Neuromag (uncorrected; 20 comparisons per array against Neuromag combined).
Benchmarks: the analytical sphere model reproduces Jas et al. (equal-SNR depth 27.665 mm at η = 3, printed about 28 mm); the Hunold adaptation reproduces the depth-orientation pattern of the published maps (r = 0.93-0.98); the Goldenholz adaptation gives a noise-source strength of 1.77 nAm (paper 1.6-1.9).

What these results do not show

  • One adult anatomy and one measured head position: no between-subject variability.
  • OPM intrinsic noise is a declared sweep (7-30 fT/√Hz), not a device specification; cross-talk is not modelled, and head motion, cap slippage and calibration errors only in a bounded extension (motion) that does not establish motion robustness.
  • Detectability is a known-topography matched-filter SNR, not a clinical detection rate; the spike study uses simulated events in simulated noise.
  • Confidence intervals resample cortical parcels or locations of one anatomy; they do not include model uncertainty, which the sensitivity analyses show instead (one factor at a time, plus a joint noise x gap grid).
  • Pediatric results rest on three average templates of one database (12, 18 and 24 months), three individual school-aged children of one dataset (7.8-8.7 years; their skull modelled, their fiducials transferred from the adult) and two scaled copies of the adult: variability between three children is shown, not estimated; no age-specific background physiology (only a bounded sensitivity), adult conductivities.
